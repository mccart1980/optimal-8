# OPTIMAL 8 · MOVE

**Twelve weeks to give you the best range of movement you can have in every joint, and to make you strong in every position you can get into. The joints are your hips, lower back, mid-back, shoulders, knees, ankles and feet, neck and wrists. Balance, landing and getting up and down are trained too, and every tendon that takes your training gets loaded on purpose. MOVE sits beside Strength, Power and Physique as a full part of the program, equal to strength, power and the engine. Every gym morning starts with it, five evenings finish with it, and Wednesday and Sunday evenings are all of it. It uses whatever works for each job, an exercise or a stretch, and every move has levels you climb and standards you test.**

The aim is range you can use and load, and a body that takes what you throw at it. Nobody is injury-proof; this is built to make you as hard to break as the evidence knows how, and to keep you moving well at 70.

---

## THE THREE LAWS

1. **Get there, own it, load it.** Every joint is trained three ways. *Get there*: long, easy holds open the range. *Own it*: holds and lifts at the very end of the range, with no help, teach your body to use it. *Load it*: strength through the whole range makes it armour. Range you can't hold yourself in is a weak spot; range you can load is protection.
2. **Every joint, every direction, every week.** Each area below is trained at least three times a week across the mornings, the sessions and the evenings. Nothing is left to chance. Where one joint covers for another (stiff hips make the lower back do their job; a stiff mid-back makes the shoulders and neck do it), both get fixed.
3. **Measured, never guessed.** Fifteen standards, tested every four weeks. Levels go up when you own them and down when the pain rule says. RANGE stays at full dose until the range standards are met.

## THE TWELVE WEEKS

Week 1 is the Monday after it goes into the app. The phase sets what's prescribed; the levels follow what you can do.

| Weeks | Phase | What it's for | Tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | **RESTORE** | Open every joint's range and own it with bodyweight. The lower back is trained still, in a straight line, with no loaded bending. Tendons start loading. | Week 1: your starting numbers |
| 5–8 | **BUILD** | Load the end of every range. The back extension goes full range. Holds get longer. | Week 5 |
| 9–12 | **OWN** | Strong in every position: heavier end-range loads, balance under load, and the Jefferson curl if you pass its gate. | Weeks 9 and 13 |

After week 12, OWN carries on at your new levels until standards 3 to 8, 12, 13 and 15 are met. Then RANGE drops to THE KEEP, 10 minutes, and everything else carries on.

**Levels.** Every exercise with levels starts at level 1 in week 1. You own a level when you do the whole prescription cleanly, with no pain over 2 out of 10, two sessions running. Then you move up. The app shows the level you're on.

## THE FIFTEEN STANDARDS

Tested in weeks 1, 5, 9 and 13, then every 8 weeks. Fourteen are done at home in about 12 minutes; the back hold is done in Sunday's gym session.

| # | Area | Standard | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Getting up | **Floor to standing.** Sit cross-legged on the floor, then stand up | 10 out of 10: lose a point for each hand, knee or lean you use |
| 2 | Balance | **One leg, eyes closed.** Arms folded | 20 s each leg |
| 3 | Hips, knees, ankles | **Deep squat.** No weight, no hands | 2 min at the bottom, heels down, chest up |
| 4 | Hips | **90/90.** Sitting bones down each side; switch sides with no hands | Both sides, and the switch |
| 5 | Hips, lower back | **Toe touch.** Feet together, knees straight | Palms flat on the floor |
| 6 | Shoulders | **Wall overhead.** Back, head and bottom against a wall, straight arms overhead | Thumbs touch the wall, lower back stays flat |
| 7 | Shoulders | **Hands behind the back.** One hand over the shoulder, the other up from below | Fingertips touch, both ways round |
| 8 | Ankles | **Knee to wall.** Foot flat, knee driven forward to touch the wall | 12 cm each side |
| 9 | Grip, shoulders | **Dead hang** | 60 s |
| 10 | Achilles | **Single-leg calf raise.** All the way up, on the flat | 25 each leg |
| 11 | Lower back | **Back hold.** On the 45-degree back extension, body straight, arms crossed | 3 min |
| 12 | Hips | **Couch position.** Back knee on a cushion against the sofa, front foot flat, buttock squeezed | 60 s each side, chest fully upright, lower back not arched |
| 13 | Mid-back | **Seated turn.** Sit on the floor, legs straight, a stick across your shoulders. Turn as far as you can each way; film it from above and read the angle | 60° each way |
| 14 | Neck | **Neck turn.** Sitting tall, turn your head as far as it goes | Chin in line with the shoulder, each way |
| 15 | Wrists | **Wrist bend.** On all fours, palms flat, fingers forward, arms straight. Move your shoulders forward past your hands | Wrists bent to a right angle, pain-free, both hands |

**Why these targets:**
- **Floor to standing:** low scores went with about 5 times the death rate at 51–80.
- **One leg:** failing a 10-second stand with eyes open went with 84% higher death risk at 51–75. 20 seconds with your eyes closed is my athlete's target.
- **Dead hang:** grip 5 kg weaker went with 16% higher death risk.
- **Calf raise:** 25 is the median for healthy adults.
- **Back hold:** under about 3 minutes on the lab version predicted back pain within a year. Ours uses the 45-degree bench, which is easier than the lab table, so 3 minutes is my target, not a lab norm.

The life-and-death figures come from studies showing a link, not cause. They're still the right targets.

## THE MORNING BLOCK — every gym morning

### MONDAY · HIPS, KNEES AND LOWER BACK · 11 min · before the box jumps (the warm-up was 5)

1. **Easy bike** — 2 min. Easy enough to talk.
2. **90/90 switches** — 2 × 5 each way. Sit on the floor, both knees bent to right angles, one leg in front with the shin across you, the other out to the side behind you. Swing both knees over to face the other way, and back. Level 1: hands on the floor behind you. Level 2: arms folded. Level 3: arms folded, and at each side lift the back foot off the floor for 3 seconds without leaning.
3. **Cossack squat** — 1 × 5 each side, 3-second pause at the bottom. Feet very wide, toes slightly out. Sit all the way down over one leg; the other leg straight, heel down and toes up. Chest up, heel of the bent leg flat. Level 1: hold a rack upright. Level 2: no support. Level 3: 8–12 kg held at your chest. Level 4: 16–20 kg. Level 5: 24 kg or more.
4. **Bird dog** — 5 each side, each held for your level's time. On all fours, back flat, as if a glass of water sat on it. Reach your right arm forward and left leg back until both are level with your body, and hold. Hand and knee back down, then the other pair. Level 1: 6-second holds. Level 2: 10 seconds. Level 3: 10 seconds, drawing small squares with the hand and the foot. It trains the back to stay still while the hips and shoulders move, with very little load on the spine.
5. **Spanish squat hold** — 3 × 45 s, hard (7 out of 10), 30 s between. A heavy band round the rack at knee height and behind your knees. Sit back into a quarter squat with your shins vertical, leaning into the band, and hold. Level 1: quarter squat. Level 2: a heavier band. Level 3: thighs level with the floor. For the knee tendons.

**In Monday's session: the standing calf raise goes heavier and slower** — 3 × 6–10, 3 seconds up and 3 seconds down, 2 seconds at the bottom stretch, one or two reps left. The same time as now, with more load on the Achilles.

### TUESDAY · LOWER BACK AND SHOULDERS · 8 min · before the throws (was 6)

1. **Bird dog** — as Monday, at your level.
2. **Cable external rotation at shoulder height** — 2 × 10–12 each arm, 2-second hold, weight logged. A cable or band at shoulder height in front of you. Upper arm out to the side at shoulder height, elbow bent to a right angle, forearm pointing forward. Rotate the forearm up until it points at the ceiling, hold 2 seconds, lower slowly. Add weight when 12 is clean.
3. **Push-up plus** — 10. A push-up; at the top, push the floor further away so your shoulder blades spread apart, then a normal rep.
4. **Two easy throws of each kind**, as now.

The band pull-aparts and band dislocates go.

### WEDNESDAY · HIPS, ANKLES AND OVERHEAD · 9 min · before the first pair (was 5)

1. **Goblet squat pry** — 2 × 30 s. Hold a kettlebell or dumbbell at your chest and sit to the bottom of a squat, heels down. Elbows inside your knees, push the knees out, and shift slowly side to side over each ankle. Level 1: hold a post with one hand. Level 2: the weight, heels flat. Level 3: 45 seconds.
2. **Prone Y-raise** — 2 × 10, 2-second hold at the top. Face down on a bench set at 30–45 degrees, very light dumbbells (start at 1–2 kg), thumbs up. Raise your arms up and out in a Y to ear height. It builds overhead strength with no load on your back.
3. **Side-lying dumbbell external rotation** — 2 × 12 each arm. Lie on your side, a rolled towel under the top elbow, elbow bent to a right angle against your side. Rotate the dumbbell up as far as you can, then lower slowly.
4. **Push-up plus** — 10, as Tuesday.
5. **A light set** of the first pair, as now.

### THURSDAY · HIPS, BALANCE AND SPRINT PREP · 12 min · before the sled (was 10)

1. **Easy bike** — 2 min (was 3).
2. **Leg swings** — 10 forward and back, 10 side to side, each leg (as now).
3. **Hip airplanes** — 2 × 4 each side. Stand on one leg. Hinge until your chest is level with the floor. Slowly turn your chest down to face the floor, then open it right up to the ceiling, the hip doing the turning. Level 1: a hand on the rack. Level 2: no hand. Level 3: a 5 kg plate held at your chest.
4. **World's greatest stretch** — 3 each side, in place of the walking lunges. A long lunge forward. Drop the same-side elbow to the inside of the front foot, then turn that arm up to the ceiling and follow it with your eyes. Step through to the other side.
5. **A-skips and the three build-ups**, as now.

**In Thursday's session: the side plank and dead bug become the Copenhagen plank** — 2 × 20–30 s each side, 30 s between. Lie on your side, propped on your forearm. Put your top leg's knee on a bench, the bottom leg underneath. Lift your hips until your body is a straight line, and hold. Level 1: knee on the bench. Level 2: ankle on the bench. It cut groin problems by 41%, and it trains the side of the trunk the side plank did.

### SATURDAY · MID-BACK, SHOULDERS AND ACHILLES · 10 min · before the throws (was 6)

1. **Elbow to the ceiling** — 6 each side. On all fours, one hand behind your head. Turn your chest and point that elbow at the ceiling, then back down. Level 2: kneeling on one knee, a light plate held at your chest, turning toward the front knee.
2. **Cable external rotation at shoulder height** — as Tuesday.
3. **Push-up plus** — 10.
4. **Single-leg calf hold** — 3 × 30 s each leg, alternating legs. Stand on one foot on a step or plate, the other hand on the rack. Rise halfway up onto your toes and hold. Level 1: bodyweight. Level 2: a 10 kg dumbbell in the same hand. Level 3: 20 kg. Level 4: 30 kg. Heavy enough that 30 seconds is hard. For the Achilles.
5. **Two easy throws of each kind**, as now.

The side plank stays in Saturday's session.

### SUNDAY · THE WHOLE BODY · 10 min · before the first pair (was 4)

1. **Turkish get-up, slow** — 2 each side; hold every position for 3 seconds. Lie on your back with a kettlebell pressed straight up in one hand, the same-side knee bent. Roll onto the other elbow, then that hand. Lift your hips, sweep the straight leg back under you to kneel, and stand up. Then reverse it back down. The bell points at the ceiling the whole way and your eyes stay on it. Level 1: no weight (a shoe balanced on your fist). Level 2: 8 kg. Level 3: 12 kg. Level 4: 16 kg. Level 5: 20 kg.
2. **Suitcase carry** — 2 × 30 m each hand. One heavy dumbbell at your side. Walk tall without leaning towards or away from it. Move up a weight when you can walk the 30 metres dead straight.
3. **Band pull-apart** — 15.
4. **A light set** of the first exercises, as now.

**In Sunday's session: the back extension goes in straight after the hammer curls**, 3 minutes more:
- **Weeks 1–4 · Back extension hold** — 3 × 30 s. On the 45-degree back extension, hips on the pad, ankles locked. Body in a straight line from head to heels, arms crossed, and hold.
- **Weeks 5–8 · Back extension, full range** — 2 × 12, 2-second hold at the top. Curl down slowly, one bit of your spine at a time, as far as you go. Then rise back up to a straight line, and no further.
- **Weeks 9–12 · Jefferson curl** — 2 × 8, only once its gate is passed. Stand on a box, legs straight, a light dumbbell held in both hands. Chin to chest, then roll down one vertebra at a time for 5 seconds, as far as your hamstrings let you, the weight hanging below your feet. Roll back up the same way in 5 seconds. Start at 5 kg; add 2.5 kg every 2 weeks while it's pain-free, up to 20 kg.
  - **The gate:** a back hold of 2 minutes or more, fingertips reaching the floor on the toe touch, and no back pain at all for 4 weeks.
  - **Gate not passed:** stay on the full-range back extension with a plate held at your chest.
- **Test weeks:** the slot is the back hold, standard 11.

The hammer curls stay as they are.

## WEDNESDAY EVENING · SESSION A · HIPS, LOWER BACK, BALANCE AND GETTING UP AND DOWN · 30 min

At home, with a mat and a chair. It replaces that night's RANGE.

1. **Settle** — 1 min. On your back, calves on a chair. In through your nose for 4, out for 8.
2. **Cat-camel** — 8 slow. On all fours. Round your back up as high as it goes, chin to chest, then let it sink and lift your head. Move one bit of the spine at a time. Evenings only (rule 3).
3. **McGill curl-up** — 5 × 10 s. Lie on your back, one knee bent and one leg straight, your hands flat under the small of your back. Lift your head and shoulders 2–3 cm as one piece, chin tucked, and hold, breathing. Swap legs halfway. Level 1: elbows on the floor. Level 2: elbows lifted. Level 3: elbows lifted, a deep breath in and out during each hold. It trains the front of the trunk without bending the spine.
4. **Side bridge** — 3 × 10 s each side. Level 1: from your knees. Level 2: from your feet. Level 3: roll from one side, through a front plank, to the other side, holding each for 10 seconds.
5. **Floor to standing** — 5 slow reps. Sit cross-legged, then stand with as little help as you can. Level 1: one hand. Level 2: no hands.
6. **One-leg balance** — 3 × 20 s each leg. Level 1: eyes open. Level 2: eyes closed. Level 3: eyes closed on a folded towel. Balance training cut injuries by about 45%.
7. **Reverse Nordic** — 2 × 6, 4 seconds down. Kneel tall on a mat, knees hip-width, body straight from knees to head, bottom squeezed. Lean back slowly, hinging only at the knees, then pull yourself back up with the front of your thighs. It loads the front of the thigh at length, the muscle that limits the couch position: 8 weeks of it lengthened that muscle's fibres in a trial. Knees grumbling over 2 out of 10: stay at the level below. Level 1: a short lean, about a quarter of the way. Level 2: halfway. Level 3: as far as you can still pull back up from.
8. **Deep squat** — 2 min at the bottom in total. Shift side to side, then reach one arm overhead, then the other. Level 1: hold a post. Level 2: hands free. Level 3: both arms overhead for the last 30 seconds.
9. **90/90 to kneeling** — 5 each side. From the 90/90, rise up onto both knees, then sit down to the other side. Level 1: hands down. Level 2: no hands. Level 3: no hands, with a 3-second hold kneeling tall.
10. **Prone hip inward turn** — a 60-second hold, then 8 lifts. Lie face down, knees together and bent to right angles, and let your feet fall out to the sides. That turns the hips inward. Hold, breathing out. Then lift both feet a little further out, hold 3 seconds, relax: 8 times. Limited inward turn is the hip measure most linked to back pain.
11. **Straight-leg lift-offs** — 2 × 8 each leg, 2-second hold. Sit tall on the floor, legs straight out in front. Lift one heel 5 cm off the floor without leaning back, hold, lower. Level 1: hands beside your hips. Level 2: hands beside your knees. Level 3: hands off the floor. Strength at the top of hip bending: the range a stretch gives you, made strong.
12. **Nerve sliders** — 10 each leg. Lie on your back and hold behind one thigh, hip bent to a right angle. Straighten the knee as you tip your head back to the floor; bend the knee as you lift your chin to your chest. Smooth, and never to a strong pull. It frees the nerve down the back of the leg, which often limits the toe touch.
13. **Frog rocks** — 10 slow, then hold the last for 30 s. On all fours, knees as wide as they go, shins parallel, feet turned out. Rock your hips back towards your heels and forward again. For the inner thighs.
14. **Pigeon** — 90 s each side. Front shin across the floor in front of you, back leg straight behind. Fold your chest down over the shin, breathing out. In weeks 9–12: 2 minutes each side, with a push-and-relax at halfway (press the front shin into the floor for 5 seconds, relax, fold further).

## SUNDAY EVENING · SESSION B · SHOULDERS, MID-BACK, NECK, WRISTS, ANKLES AND FEET · 30 min

At home, with the bar, a mat, a stick (a broom handle) and a dumbbell. It replaces that night's RANGE.

1. **Settle** — 1 min, as Session A.
2. **Neck range with pushes** — each way: turn left, turn right, ear to the left shoulder, ear to the right shoulder, chin to chest. Sitting tall, go as far as you can. Put your hand against your head and push into it for 5 seconds without moving, relax, then go a little further. Hold the new end for 5 seconds. Slow and easy; never force the neck.
3. **Wrist rocks and palm lifts** — on all fours, palms flat. Rock forward and back 10 times with your fingers pointing forward, then 10 with your fingers turned back towards your knees. Then fingers forward: lift your palms off the floor, keeping your fingers down, 10 times. For push-ups, bench and the handstand.
4. **Seated turn** — 6 each way. Sit on the floor, legs crossed, the stick across your shoulders. Turn as far as you can and hold 3 seconds. Then try to turn a little further with no help from your arms, and hold 3 more. Standard 13.
5. **Chest lifts** — 2 × 8, 3-second hold. Lie face down, hands by your forehead, elbows out. Lift your chest and elbows 3 cm off the floor, keeping your neck long, and hold. Mid-back strength in its arch.
6. **Overhead lift-offs** — 2 × 8, 3-second hold. Lie face down, straight arms overhead on the floor in a Y, thumbs up. Lift both arms off the floor and hold. Standard 6.
7. **Turn-out lifts** — 2 × 8 each arm, 3-second hold. Lie face down, arm out to the side at shoulder height, elbow bent to a right angle, forearm hanging down off a bed or bench edge. Rotate the forearm up as far as it goes and hold. Level 2: a 1 kg weight in the hand.
8. **Turn-in lifts** — 2 × 8 each arm, 3-second hold. Stand, put the back of one hand on your lower back. Lift the hand off your back without moving your body, and hold. Standard 7, made strong.
9. **Behind-the-back shoulder stretch** — 60 s. Sit on the floor, hands on a bench or step behind you, fingers pointing back. Slide your hips forward and let your chest lift. The front of the shoulders.
10. **Dead hang** — 2 min in total, in as few sets as you can. Standard 9.
11. **Loaded knee to wall** — 60 s each side. Half-kneel facing a wall, front foot a hand's length from it, a dumbbell resting on your front thigh just above the knee. Drive the knee forward over the toes, heel down, and hold. Level 1: no weight. Level 2: 10 kg. Level 3: 20 kg. Standard 8.
12. **Single-leg calf raise** — 2 × 15 each leg, all the way up and all the way down off a step, 1 second at the top. Level 2: a 10 kg dumbbell in the same hand. Standard 10.
13. **Kettlebell arm bar** — 35 s each arm. Lie on your back, a light kettlebell pressed straight up in one hand, that knee bent. Roll onto your other side and slowly straighten the bent leg behind you until you're lying on your front. The bell keeps pointing at the ceiling, arm straight, shoulder pulled down away from your ear. Breathe there, then roll back and swap arms.

## RANGE · five evenings a week · 20 min · re-aimed

Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday. Same order and same holds as now, with five cards out and three in. Every card not described here is described in the home-routines document.

**Out:**
- **Thread the needle** — the open book does its job.
- **Squat-to-stand** — it rounds the lower back while you hold your toes.
- **Sleeper stretch** — in a 4-week trial it was no better than doing nothing.
- **Wall slides** — the lowest value on the page.
- **Light band rotations** — they're in the gym now, with real load.

**In:**
- **Hamstring stretch in a doorway** — 60 s each side, after the couch stretch. Lie on your back in a doorway, one leg straight up the door frame, the other straight along the floor through the doorway. Shuffle closer until you feel a clear stretch behind the thigh. At halfway: press your heel into the frame for 5 seconds, relax, shuffle closer. Your spine stays flat on the floor.
- **Cross-body stretch** — 45 s each side, in place of the sleeper stretch. Lie on your back. Bring one arm across your chest at shoulder height. With the other hand, pull it in by the elbow towards the opposite shoulder. At halfway: push the arm back against your hand for 5 seconds, relax, pull further. In a 4-week trial it gained 20° of shoulder rotation, against 12° for the sleeper stretch and 6° for nothing.
- **Doorway chest stretch** — 45 s each side, in place of the wall slides. Forearm flat on the door frame, elbow at shoulder height. Step through with the same-side foot until your chest stretches. At halfway: press the forearm into the frame for 5 seconds, relax, step further.

**How hard: 3 to 5 out of 10.** A clear stretch you can breathe through slowly, never forced. Low-intensity stretching built more usable range than hard stretching did.

**RANGE stays at 20 minutes until the range standards (3 to 8, 12, 13 and 15) are met.** It doesn't drop to THE KEEP at its week 13 in mid-January.

## THE RULES

1. **The pain rule.** Score any pain 0 to 10.
   - 0–2: carry on.
   - 3–4: stay on the level; don't move up.
   - 5 or more, or worse the next morning: that exercise drops a level for a week.
   - Sharp, pinching, or pain going into a leg or arm: stop that exercise for the day.
   - The same pain two weeks running: see a physio.
   
   In a tendon trial, carrying on with loading under low, monitored pain did as well as resting.
2. **Get help at once.** Back pain with any of these means A&E straight away:
   - numbness between the legs or around the bottom or genitals
   - a change in bladder or bowel control
   - weakness in the legs
   
   Pain or pins and needles below the knee or down the arm: see your GP or a physio before more back or neck work.
3. **No end-range back bending in the first hour after waking.** Cat-camel, the Jefferson curl and any bending forward to the floor wait until you've been up an hour. That's why the morning five goes: its cat-camel came first thing. Teaching back-pain patients to avoid early-morning bending cut their pain by about a third, in a small trial.
4. **A pinch is not a stretch.** If the deep squat, the 90/90 or the couch position pinches at the front of the hip, or an overhead position pinches at the top of the shoulder, stop going deeper there and see a physio. That's the joint, and stretching won't change it.
5. **The neck is never forced.** Slow, easy range, and pushes into your own hand only.
6. **Red mornings.** The movement block still runs; it's the part that helps. The Spanish squat and calf holds go at 5 out of 10, not 7.
7. **Lighter and test weeks in Strength, Power and Physique.** MOVE runs as written.
8. **After an injury.** RESTORE is built for it. Start at level 1 and let the pain rule decide; don't jump phases early.

### What to know about your back

- **Your spine is built to bend, twist and carry load.** Like muscle and tendon, it gets stronger when load is built up step by step. That is what the three phases do.
- **Bending isn't the enemy.** Bending your back while lifting hasn't been shown to cause back pain; building up to it is what matters.
- **How you think about your back changes how it does.** A treatment built on dropping fearful beliefs and guarded, braced movement (cognitive functional therapy) cut back disability far more than usual care, and the gain lasted a year. Move with confidence inside the pain rule.
- **Exercise plus knowing this is the combination that prevents back pain:** 45% fewer episodes in the first year.

## WHAT TO EXPECT

- **Weeks 1–4:** the positions start to feel easier. Most early range is your nervous system letting you go further, not longer muscle.
- **Weeks 5–12:** usable range climbs as the end-range strength comes in.

- **Last to come:** the toe touch, the wall overhead, the couch position and the seated turn. A joint limited by its bone won't change, which is what rule 4 is for.

## WHY IT'S BUILT THIS WAY

- **Stretching builds range, and the method matters less than doing it.** Static holds and push-and-relax beat moving stretches for lasting range. Beyond a modest dose (about 5 minutes a week per muscle, most days), more time hasn't been shown to add much.
- **Full-range lifting builds as much range as stretching,** and eccentric training builds flexibility.
- **Strength is specific to the position you train it in.** Training at long muscle lengths carries over to more joint angles, and in a trial, strength at the end of the range built more usable range than hard stretching. Hence the lift-offs, the end-range holds and the loaded Cossacks.
- **Exercise prevents injuries; stretching doesn't.**
  - Strength training cut sports injuries to about a third, and balance training cut them by about 45%. Stretching made no difference.
  - For the back: exercise with education cut back-pain episodes by 45% in the first year, and belts, insoles and education alone did nothing.
  - In chronic back pain, strength and motor-control training gave the best function, while stretching and McKenzie exercises were no better than nothing.
- **The back gets strong in every position, step by step.** Bending your back while lifting hasn't been shown to cause back pain, so the program builds bending strength in stages:
  1. The curl-up, side bridge and bird dog give high stability with low spine load.
  2. Hard lower-back extension training helped chronic back pain, even once a week.
  3. Then the Jefferson curl.
  
  Low back-extensor endurance predicted back pain within a year.
- **Joints cover for each other.** Limited inward hip turn is the hip measure most linked to back pain, though the evidence is weak.
- **Specific exercises protect specific injuries:** the Copenhagen for the groin (41% fewer problems), Nordics for the hamstrings (about half), and a shoulder rotation warm-up for the shoulders (28% fewer).
- **Tendons need heavy load:** 70% effort or more, over about 14 weeks. They still respond at 74. In a UK study of Achilles ruptures, the typical patient was 45, 79% were men, and 65% happened in sport.
- **Long holds just before power work cost about 4.6%,** so the gym mornings are movement and short holds, and the long holds are in the evening.
- **Range gains hold in active people,** which is why THE KEEP waits until you've arrived.
