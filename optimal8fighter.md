# OPTIMAL 8 — FIGHTER · v1.5

**The fighter's build. Four weekday mornings of about an hour, Friday asleep, Saturday 90 minutes, Sunday 75. Calisthenics threaded through the week where each piece belongs. RANGE — twenty minutes of real mobility work — at home every evening, the joint circles every morning. The mind trained inside the sessions, where it's tested. Nothing in the gym that doesn't make you a better fighter.**

Your strength and conditioning for 16 weeks. Your coach owns boxing. This owns everything else: maximal strength, punching power, speed, the engine, and the tissue that keeps you training. Power is dosed three mornings a week, the engine is built twice, the squat gets a second exposure, the trunk is braced, the landmine appears twice. Boxing runs separately.

Week 1 began Monday 7 September. Switch to these pages from your next session — the week table is identical to the Singles version, so nothing restarts and no number changes.

Everything you need to run a session is written on that session's page. No codes, no cross-referencing, no jargon. If a word needed explaining, it's explained where it appears.

**What changed in v1.1, and why.** Friday's easy ride is gone and Friday is a sleep day — the 3:30 alarm bought 55 minutes of easy spinning and cost 75 minutes of sleep the night before the biggest session of the week; that trade was never worth making. A tendon block is in — two short isometric holds a week — because strength that outruns tendons is the injury that ends a cycle, and the sprints, depth jumps and Nordics are where it would happen. The mental drills that happen inside sessions (round-recovery breathing, the 60-second settle, the silent sled, the post-max sit, the nasal threshold) are now written on the session pages and run from this app, not from a second document. And there is a CAMP MODE page for when boxing returns, because a program that only works while you're not boxing isn't a boxer's program.

**What changed in v1.2.** Calisthenics is in — as pieces placed where they belong, not as a session of its own. Ring dips and the muscle-up line on Monday with the rest of the pressing and pulling; ring rows on Wednesday; the pistol line on Tuesday after the jumps; L-sits and one lever hold on Sunday; the handstand and the hollow at home, four evenings a week, because balance skills are built by frequency, not by one long session. Nothing was dropped to make room, and the weekdays now run to about an hour rather than under it — a few minutes over is the correct price for the shoulders, trunk and single-leg control this adds. Every line has levels and an "own it when" on the Calisthenics page. Friday is still empty.

**What changed in v1.4.** Three things you asked for. The evening mobility block becomes RANGE — twenty minutes a night for twelve weeks aimed at the two joints you named, hips and shoulders, through the mid-back that limits both, with long holds, contract-relax, and strength at the end of the range, measured by four tests every four weeks; after twelve weeks it drops to a ten-minute keep. Five minutes of controlled joint circles on waking, every day. And the functional pieces: the Turkish get-up opens the Saturday and Sunday warm-ups, bear crawls open Tuesday's and Thursday's. About ten minutes across the week in the gym and fifteen a night at home over what was there. Nothing was removed. (Rests, the fast-bar ceiling and the nasal-test weeks are as they were in v1.2 — your call on those is still open.)

**What changed in v1.5 — after the final examination.** Two engine changes you asked for: the 30-second all-outs are replaced by repeat bursts — the session shaped most like a round, and the one the week was missing — and thirty to forty easy nasal minutes on a weekend afternoon put the aerobic base back where it costs no sleep. Then five things the examination found and I've fixed because they were errors, not preferences: the pause-squat percentages were written against the back-squat max and had you doing paused triples at 84% — near a paused maximum, alone, at 4am — so they're now 65–75%; Monday's box jumps had no lower-body warm-up in front of them, now they do; the Achilles hold is now standing on a straight knee, which is where the Achilles actually lives; the Saturday squat warm-up sets now happen before the squat rather than forty minutes earlier; Thursday's throws get three easy throws first, as Sunday's already did. And one placement change: the Nordics move ahead of the fight simulation, so the hardest eccentric work of the week is done on fresh hamstrings, not after twenty-four minutes of rounds. Everything else the examination recommends is listed in the examination, as decisions for you.

**What left, and why.** The size blocks — lateral raises, rear-delt flies, shrugs, pulldowns, dips, curls — were 42 minutes a week that built the look, not the fighter. They're gone. The bench, push press, weighted chins, rows, trap bar and throws carry the shoulders, traps and arms from here. Mobility moved to the evening at home. Nothing else went: every sprint, jump, throw, lift, round and durability drill from the Singles version is still here, at the same numbers.

**How to read a session page.** Each page is a running order, numbered 1, 2, 3… with a clock time next to each step. Start at the top, work down, done. Every exercise shows: sets × reps · the weight · the rest between sets · a "How" line telling you exactly what to do.

**How the weights work.** Every barbell weight is a percentage of the most you can lift once (your "max").

You already know two: squat and bench. You find the other two in week 1: trap bar deadlift (Wednesday) and push press (Saturday). How: put light weight on, do 3 reps, add 10–20 kg, do 3 more. Keep going until 3 reps is hard but you had two more in you. Take that weight, add 8%, and that's your max. Example: hard triple at 150 kg → 150 × 1.08 = 162 kg.

Worked example all the way through this document: squat max 140 kg. Week 1 says squat 4 × 6 @ 75%. 75% of 140 = 105 kg. That's it. Round to the nearest 2.5 kg.

After a max-single week or test day, your new number becomes the max, and every percentage moves up with it.

**THE FAST-BAR RULE — new, and it matters.** A percentage of a number measured weeks ago goes stale fast when you're getting stronger quickly. So on the four main lifts — squat, bench, trap bar, push press — after your last work set, ask one question: *did the last rep move as fast as the first?* If yes, raise that lift's max by 2.5% before next week (squat and trap bar: round to the nearest 5 kg; bench and push press: 2.5 kg). Once per lift per week, no more. In the app, that's the max number in Settings. Max-single weeks and test day replace the number outright. The grind rule still runs the other way: a grinding rep ends the set, and two grinding sets on one lift in a week means take 2.5% back off. If a lift hits the fast-bar rule three weeks running, expect the week-4 or week-9 max to come in well above 102% — take the second attempt.

**Rests.** Shorter than the Singles version on every strength lift: 2 minutes on the main lifts (2½ on the heavy triples in weeks 7–9 and on max days), 75–90 seconds on the accessories. Speed, jumps, sprints and throws keep their full rests — those are trained by quality, and a short rest turns them into conditioning. If a strength set feels flat after 2 minutes, take 2½. The number on the page is the default, not a stopwatch you fail.

**Your engine number.** Sunday of week 1 and Sunday of week 16 replace the fight rounds with a 20-minute bike test (written on the Sunday page). It gives you two things: the only measurement of the engine this program builds, and your peak heart rate — the number every easy ride is paced off.

**Which week am I in?** Count from your start Monday. The tables on the next page give you every number for every week. Weeks 5 and 10 are easy weeks (deloads). Weeks 15–16 wind down. Saturday of week 16 is test day.

**The daily check — do this before every session.** Ask four things: Is my resting heart rate up? Do I feel unusually sore? Flat and unmotivated? Sleep quality worse than normal for me?

- 0–1 yes = GREEN. Train as written.
- 2–3 yes = YELLOW. Take 7% off every barbell weight. Sprints become 3 runs at 90% effort, never flat out. Skip the last section of the session.
- Ill, injured, or 3 yellow days in a row = RED. No heavy lifts, no sprints, no jumps. Do the warm-up, the neck and shoulder work, then go home. Mobility at home as normal.

**The head-contact rule.** The morning after any sparring you do the LIGHT SESSION — Tuesday's warm-up, Thursday's neck block, Monday's shoulder circuit, about 30 minutes — or nothing. No heavy lifting, no sprinting, no jumping for 24 hours after head contact. The app swaps it in with one tap. Any headache, fogginess or light sensitivity: do nothing, and tell your coach.

**The output rule.** Every explosive set ends the moment the output drops — jump height, throw distance, bar speed. Rep counts are ceilings, not targets.

## TRAINING ALONE — NON-NEGOTIABLE

- **Squat:** safety pins in the rack, set just below your lowest position, on every set over 80%. No pins in the gym = no max singles; stop at a heavy 3.
- **Bench:** pins at chest height, and never clamp collars on the bar — if you get stuck you tip the plates off. Bench max singles only happen on Sundays, fresh, first thing.
- **Trap bar at 3am:** the one heavy lift allowed at that hour. Nothing passes over your body, and a rep you're not sure of gets set down, not fought.
- **Nordics:** heels anchored under something solid. Stop the set the moment your lower back rounds.
- **Drop jumps:** rubber hex dumbbells, drop them straight down, land on clear floor.
- If you are not certain you'll make the rep, you don't start it.

## MONDAY · 3AM · UPPER BODY STRENGTH + POWER DOSE + RINGS · ~65 min

Upper strength and the first of the week's three power doses. Nothing is taken to failure.

**1 · 0:00 — WARM-UP · 8 min**

Lie on your back, feet up on a bench, knees at right angles. 5 slow breaths: in through the nose for 4 seconds, out for 8.

Band pull-aparts × 20 — hold a light band in front of you at shoulder height, arms straight, pull it out until it touches your chest, control it back.

Band external rotations × 15 per arm — elbow pinned to your ribs, bent 90°, hand rotates outward against the band.

Wall slides × 10 — back flat on a wall, forearms on the wall like a goalpost, slide them up and down without them leaving the wall.

Bench warm-up sets: bar × 10, 40% × 5, 60% × 3, 75% × 2. Rest about a minute between.

**2 · 0:08 — BENCH THROW · 5 sets of 3 (week 16: 3 sets of 3) · start ~1/3 of bench max · rest 2 min · 11 min**

How: on a Smith machine, bar light. Lower to your chest, then press so hard the bar leaves your hands at the top. Catch it, reset, repeat. The right weight is whatever flies highest — adjust until it does.

Stop the set the instant a throw is visibly lower than the last.

No Smith machine? Throw a 4–6 kg medicine ball off your chest at a wall, 5 × 5, as hard as you can.

This is your punch-speed lift. In weeks 11–14 it merges into the circuit below.

**3 · 0:19 — POWER DOSE · BOX JUMPS · 3 sets of 3 · rest 60–90s · 6 min**

First, one minute of legs — the warm-up so far was all shoulders: pogo hops × 20 (bounce on the balls of the feet, legs almost straight), bodyweight squats × 6, two easy jumps onto a low step.

How: choose the box by the landing, not the height — you should land on it in a quarter squat, knees soft, not folded to your chest. The training is the take-off; a higher box only makes you lift your knees faster. Quick dip, jump as high as you can, land soft on top, step down. Three perfect jumps, never three tired ones.

Explosiveness responds to how often the nervous system is asked, not how much. Five minutes, three mornings a week.

**4 · 0:25 — BENCH PRESS · sets, reps and % from the week table · rest 2 min (weeks 7–9: 2½) · 10 min**

How: pins at chest height, no collars. Controlled down, drive up hard. If a rep grinds, that set is over. Last set: fast-bar rule — last rep as fast as the first, add 2.5% to your bench max for next week.

Weeks 11–14 only, steps 2 and 4 merge into one circuit, 4 rounds: 2 bench reps @ 85% → rest 20s → 3 bench throws → rest 20s → 3 clap push-ups → rest 2½ min. Heavy wakes the system up; fast uses it.

**5 · 0:35 — RING DIPS · 3 sets at your level (Calisthenics page) · rest 90s · 6 min**

How, at the level most men start: support yourself at the top of a pair of rings, arms locked, rings still, then lower until your shoulders are level with your elbows and press back up, turning the rings so your palms face forward at the top. If you can't yet, the level below is the bar dip, and below that the support hold. Elbow pain of any kind is a stop sign, not a challenge.

Pressing is grouped on this morning on purpose — bench throw, bench, dips — so that the rest of the week's shoulders stay fresh for throws and push press. The rings are what make the shoulder bulletproof: every rep is the joint stabilising itself in three directions at once.

**6 · 0:41 — WEIGHTED CHIN-UPS · 3 sets of 5, then 2 sets of the MUSCLE-UP LINE at your level · rest 90s · 9 min**

How: wide grip, palms away (switch to a neutral grip if wide ever bothers your shoulders), weight on a belt or a dumbbell between your feet. Start from a dead hang, pull chin over the bar, lower under control, no swinging. Add weight when all three sets are clean.

Then the muscle-up line, bodyweight, 2 sets: at the first level that's chest-to-bar pull-ups — explosive, the bar touches the chest, elbows driving down and back — and it climbs through muscle-up negatives to the strict bar muscle-up and the ring muscle-up (Calisthenics page). It's a skill, not a grind: two crisp sets, never a third.

Pulling strength protects the shoulders that throw a thousand punches a week; the muscle-up is pulling power, the same quality that snaps a hand back.

**7 · 0:50 — CHEST-SUPPORTED ROW · 4 sets of 8 (easy weeks 3) · rest 75s · 7 min**

How: lie chest-down on an incline bench with dumbbells. Row until your elbows pass your body, pause one second at the top, lower slow.

Four sets so your pulling matches your pressing. A boxer's shoulders live or die on that balance.

**8 · 0:57 — SHOULDER CIRCUIT + BAND CATCH · 2 rounds · 8 min**

Round = the four moves back to back, then rest 45s:

Side-lying external rotation × 12 per side — lie on your side, top elbow glued to your ribs, rotate a light dumbbell from your belly up toward the ceiling.

Prone T raise × 10 — lie face down on an incline bench, arms hanging; raise them out to a T shape, thumbs up.

Face pulls × 15 — rope on a high cable, pull it to your face with elbows high, squeeze the back of the shoulders.

Wall slides × 10 — same as the warm-up.

Then BAND DECELERATION CATCH · 2 sets of 8 per arm: hold a light band anchored behind you. Punch the arm out fast; the band snatches it back — brake it hard and stop it dead in the last third of the return. Fast out, hard stop.

Every right hand you throw, this muscle group has to brake your arm. This is the only place it gets trained to.

**Done · ~1:05.** Tonight at home: RANGE, then the skill block (the home page).

## TUESDAY · 3AM · CRAWLS · JUMPS · PISTOLS · ENGINE 1 · HANDS · TRUNK · ACHILLES · ~65 min

Power dose first, fresh, then the first of the week's two engine sessions. Your legs had yesterday off.

**1 · 0:00 — WARM-UP · 8 min**

3 easy minutes on the bike → band pull-aparts × 20 → goblet squats × 8 (hold a dumbbell at your chest, squat) → push-ups × 10 → 90/90 hip switches × 5 each way (sit with one leg bent in front of you, one bent behind, both at right angles; swivel the knees over to the other side without using your hands) → pogo hops × 20 (bounce on the balls of your feet, legs almost straight, quick off the floor).

Then BEAR CRAWLS · 2 min — on hands and feet, knees an inch off the floor and staying there, back flat as a table, opposite hand and foot moving together, small steps. 10 metres forward, 10 metres backward, four times. Slow beats fast: if the hips rock or the knees lift, you've gone too quick. Loads the shoulder and trunk in a pattern the body hasn't done since you were two, and it's the bridge between the handstand and the get-up.

**2 · 0:08 — POWER DOSE · JUMPS · 8 min · rest 90s**

Broad jumps, 3 × 2 — two-foot jump forward for distance, stick the landing dead still.

Trap bar jumps, 3 × 3 — stand inside a trap bar loaded to 20% of your trap bar max (week 1: empty bar), jump with it, land soft.

The second of three weekly power doses. Fresh legs, before the bike — never after.

**3 · 0:16 — THE PISTOL LINE · 2 sets of 5 per leg at your level · rest 60s · 5 min**

How, at the level most men start: stand on one leg in front of a box or bench, the other leg held straight out in front, sit down to the box under control and stand back up without touching the floor with the free foot. The line climbs through the assisted pistol (fingertips on a post) to the full pistol, then the weighted pistol, with the shrimp squat as a rotation (Calisthenics page). Two sets, never to failure, never sore: Saturday's sprints are four days away, Wednesday's heavy lower is tomorrow, and this block is control, not load.

The split squat on Thursday is the loaded single-leg strength. This is the unloaded single-leg skill — balance, ankle, knee tracking, the pivot foot learning to own the whole body.

**4 · 0:21 — BIKE SESSION · 24 min · check the week table — Tuesday's column**

Bike or SkiErg — pick one and keep it all 16 weeks so your numbers compare. Write down your output.

4-minute intervals: 4 rounds of 4 minutes HARD (breathing heavily, could speak 2–3 words at most) with 3 easy minutes between. The single best session there is for building your engine.

40-second repeats: 40 seconds absolutely flat out, 80 seconds easy. Do 3, rest 5 full minutes, do 3 more. Rounds 1–2 feel fine, round 3 burns, the last three are horrible. That burning is the point — it's what the late rounds of a fight feel like, and this is the only session that goes there. Don't hold back early to survive the end.

Repeat bursts: 8 bursts of 6–8 seconds at absolute maximum, with 40 seconds easy spinning between them; then 5 full minutes easy; then 8 more. Two sets, sixteen bursts. The first three feel like nothing. By the sixth the burst is still there but it costs; by the eighth of the second set the question is whether the burst is still a burst. That's the point — this is a flurry, twenty or thirty seconds apart, for a round, and what decides whether the fourth flurry has anything in it is how fast the muscle refills between them, which is an aerobic job done at flat-out intensity. The one session in the week shaped exactly like the thing you do in a ring. Rule: if a burst is visibly weaker than the last, take an extra 20 seconds; if two in a row are, the set is over.

Easy: 20 minutes at a pace where you could chat. That's it.

**5 · 0:45 — THE 60-SECOND SETTLE · 1 min**

Stay on the bike after the last interval. Eyes closed, heart pounding, chest heaving — find the breath at the nostrils and stay on it. Count how many seconds it takes to land there and write it down. This is the corner between rounds, trained, for nothing, every Tuesday and Thursday.

**6 · 0:46 — HANDS · 5 min**

Knuckle hold, 3 × 20 seconds — push-up position on your fists on a mat, wrist dead straight so forearm and knuckles make one line. Hold. Move to a firmer surface over the weeks only if your wrist stays straight.

Band wrist extension, 2 × 15 — forearm on your knee, palm down, band in the hand; lift the knuckles up toward you.

The most common boxing injury is a wrist folding under impact. Five minutes of insurance. Your job covers your grip — no grip work in here.

**7 · 0:51 — TRUNK · 9 min · rest 45s between exercises**

Pallof press, 3 × 10 per side — band at chest height, anchored beside you. Press your hands straight out and hold 2 seconds without letting it twist you. Anti-rotation: your trunk is what turns leg drive into hand speed, and it has to be stiff to transmit it.

Ab wheel rollouts, 3 × 8–12 — knees down, roll the wheel out only as far as your lower back stays flat, pull back.

Copenhagen plank, 2 × 30 seconds per side — side plank with your top foot up on a bench, bottom leg lifted off the floor. Hold. Groin strength — the muscles you pivot off.

**8 · 1:00 — SEATED CALF RAISE + ACHILLES HOLD · 3 sets of 12, then one 45-second hold · rest 60s · 5 min**

How: seated machine, or a barbell padded across your knees. Up on the balls of the feet, pause, down slow, twelve times — seated, because the knee-bent position trains the muscle that keeps you on your toes in round six. Then the hold is STANDING: on the edge of a step, both feet, a dumbbell in each hand or a bar on your back, as heavy as you can hold dead still — rise to the top and hold 45 seconds, knees straight, no bouncing, no sinking. Straight knee, because that is the position the Achilles takes every sprint and every landing; a seated hold loads a different muscle into the same tendon and does less for it.

The hold is the tendon block, part one. Muscle gets strong in weeks; tendon gets stiff in months, and a heavy still hold is the best-proven way to hurry it. Your Achilles takes every sprint and every depth jump on Saturday. Forty-five seconds a week is the insurance.

**Done · ~1:05.** Tonight at home: RANGE, then the skill block.

## WEDNESDAY · 3AM · SLED · TRAP BAR · PAUSE SQUAT · RDL · NECK HOLDS · RING ROWS · ~60 min

The heavy lower morning. Sled first while the nervous system is freshest, then the pulls, then the second squat exposure. Nothing to failure. The hamstring work that leaves you sore is on Sunday, not here. Sparred last night? This becomes the light session, and the heavy work waits a week.

**1 · 0:00 — WARM-UP · 6 min** — Tuesday's warm-up, then trap bar warm-up sets (bar × 5, 50% × 3, 65% × 2) and squat warm-up sets (40% × 3, 60% × 2).

**2 · 0:06 — HEAVY SLED SPRINTS · 5 runs (week 1: 4; easy weeks 3) · rest 2½–3 min · 16 min**

How: load the sled with 40–60% of your bodyweight (32–48 kg at 80 kg). Lean into it at about 45°, and sprint 20 metres driving the ground backwards through your whole foot.

Get the load right by the clock: each 20m run should take 5–7 seconds. Faster — add weight. Slower — take some off.

This is forward pressure — your fighting style with resistance on it. First in the session, before the lifts, while the nervous system is freshest.

**THE SILENT SLED — when it's the week's test.** No music, count your exhales through every run. Write down two numbers: the exhale on which attention broke, and the run on which the legs broke. The gap between them is your Crossover gap — where your mind quits against where your body quits. It rotates with the other tests (Sunday's post-max sit, the Thursday nasal threshold, the countback hold, cold with an anchor); one a week, whichever the app says.

**3 · 0:22 — TRAP BAR DEADLIFT · sets, reps and % from the week table · rest 2 min (weeks 7–9: 2½) · 12 min**

How: stand inside the bar, grip the handles, flat back, drive the floor away FAST. No grinding, no slow reps — set the bar down and end the set if speed drops. Week 1: no percentages yet — this is where you find your 3RM (the ramp is on the Start Here page). Hard triple × 1.08 = your max. Last set: fast-bar rule.

At 3am this is the one heavy lift allowed: nothing passes over your body, and a rep that isn't there gets set down, not fought.

**4 · 0:34 — PAUSE SQUAT · sets, reps and % from the week table · rest 2 min · PINS SET · 10 min**

How: bar on your back, sit to just below parallel and hold there, dead still, for a full two seconds — then drive up hard. Pins set.

Weeks 11–14 the pause goes and it becomes a SPEED SQUAT: 3 × 3 @ 70%, every rep as fast as you can move it. Skipped on max-single weeks (4, 9) and in week 16.

This is the second squat exposure — submaximal, paused, from the position rear-leg drive starts in. Saturday owns the heavy squat; this is at a weight you own.

**5 · 0:44 — ROMANIAN DEADLIFT · 3 × 6 · rest 75s · 6 min**

## THE HOME BLOCK · THE MORNING FIVE · RANGE · THE SKILL BLOCK

Two joints you named as your limiting factor: hips and shoulders. Both are limited first by the mid-back, so it goes first. Eight minutes a night was maintenance; this is change. Twenty minutes every evening for twelve weeks, five on waking, four tests every four weeks so the range is a number, then a ten-minute keep for life. Kit: a foam roller, a light band, a kettlebell or dumbbell (8–16 kg), a doorframe pull-up bar if you can fit one, a sofa and a chair.

**How range changes — so you do it right.** Stiffness is partly the tissue and mostly the nervous system guarding it. Three things move it, and the eight-minute block did none of them: long holds — ninety seconds to three minutes, breathing slowly out through the nose, because a long exhale is what tells the body it's safe to let go; contract-relax — pushing gently into the stretch for five seconds at a third of your strength, then relaxing and sinking further, three times, which resets the guard directly; and strength at the end of the range, because the body only keeps range it can control. Every joint below gets all three. Never into pain; into a strong stretch, and stay.

**One line on the hips, worth reading twice.** A stretch should pull at the back of the hip, the groin or the front of the thigh. If a deep squat or the 90/90 *pinches* at the front of the hip, or one side is markedly worse than the other, that's a joint pattern, not a muscle one — get it looked at before the twelve weeks, and we work around it.

### RANGE · every evening · 20 min · twelve weeks, then the keep

**0 · Down-regulate · 1 min** — lie on your back, feet on a chair, five slow breaths: in through the nose for 4, out for 8. Range work on a wound-up nervous system is wasted.

**1 · MID-BACK · 5 min — first, always**

Foam roller extensions — roller across the mid-back, hands behind head, arch back over it × 8, moving up a notch each time; then find the stiffest notch and stay there, 45 seconds, breathing out.

Open book, 90 seconds per side — lie on your side, knees bent and stacked, arms together out front; open the top arm to the far side, chest following, knees glued down. Hold 3 seconds at the end, come back, again. Contract-relax: at the end of the range, press the top hand gently *toward the ceiling* against nothing for 5 seconds, relax, open further.

Thread the needle × 8 per side — on all fours, slide one arm under the other, shoulder toward the floor, hold 3 seconds.

Loaded rotation × 6 per side — side-lying windmill: on your side, knees bent, a light dumbbell in the top hand held straight up; keeping the knees down, arc the weight over to the far side and back. The weight is what makes the range yours.

**2 · HIPS · 8 min**

90/90, 2 minutes per side — sit with the front leg bent at a right angle in front of you, the back leg bent at a right angle behind, both sitting bones on the floor. First 60 seconds: fold the chest over the front shin, breathing out — that's external rotation. Contract-relax: press the front shin into the floor for 5 seconds, relax, fold further, three times. Then the internal-rotation lift, 8 reps: sit tall, and lift the back foot off the floor without leaning, hold 3 seconds, lower. It'll barely move at first. This lift is the single most important movement on this page for a hook and a cross — it's the hip learning to turn under control.

Couch stretch, 90 seconds per side — back foot up on the sofa behind you, knee down on a cushion, front foot forward, squeeze the glute, stand tall. Contract-relax: drive the back knee into the cushion for 5 seconds, relax, stand taller.

Cossack squats × 6 per side, 3-second pause at the bottom — feet very wide, sit all the way down over one leg, the other straight with the heel down. The loaded end-range for the groin.

Deep squat hold, 90 seconds — a weight at the chest, sit in the very bottom, elbows prying the knees out, heels down, breathing out. Then squat-to-stand × 5 — from standing, fold and hold your toes, pull down into the full squat with the chest up, then straighten the legs while keeping hold of the toes, and back down.

Hip airplanes × 5 per side — stand on one leg holding a chair, hinge the chest to horizontal, rotate the chest toward the floor and then up to the ceiling. Active control at the end of the range.

**3 · SHOULDERS · 6 min**

Hang, 60–90 seconds — from a doorframe bar, feet on the floor as much as you need, shoulders relaxed up by the ears, breathing out. No bar: hands on a table, step back, hinge and let the chest sink between the arms, 90 seconds.

Prayer stretch, 90 seconds — kneel in front of a chair, elbows on the seat, hands behind your head, sink the chest toward the floor. Contract-relax: press the elbows into the seat for 5 seconds, relax, sink. Overhead range, and the lats that steal it.

Sleeper stretch, 60 seconds per side — lie on your side, bottom arm straight out in front bent at a right angle, forearm pointing at the ceiling; with the other hand press the forearm gently toward the floor. Internal rotation, the range that keeps a guard from riding up. Contract-relax: push the forearm up into your hand for 5 seconds, relax, press further.

Kettlebell arm bar, 45 seconds per side — lie on your back, a light kettlebell pressed up in the right hand, right knee bent; roll onto your left side and slowly straighten the right leg behind you until you're lying on your front with the bell still pointing at the ceiling, arm straight, shoulder packed. Breathe there. The loaded end-range for the shoulder, and the best opener there is for a chest and mid-back that have closed.

Wall slides × 12, with a 3-second reach at the top — back flat on the wall, forearms on the wall like a goalpost, slide up without them leaving the wall. Band external rotations × 15 per arm. The range you just found, put under control.

**After twelve weeks — THE KEEP · 10 min.** The roller extensions, open book, the 90/90 with the lift, the couch stretch, the deep squat hold, the hang and the arm bar. Every evening, for good. The tests every eight weeks.

### THE SKILL BLOCK · Monday to Thursday · 6 min · after RANGE, before the sit

Balance skills are built by frequency. Five minutes of handstand four evenings a week beats an hour of it on one; the wall in your house is a better teacher than any gym. Wrists first, always: circles × 10 each way, then palms flat on the floor and rock forward and back × 10.

Handstand, 4 minutes at your level (Calisthenics page) — at the first level, wall walk-ups: hands on the floor, feet on the wall, walk the hands back toward the wall until the chest is near it, hold ten seconds, walk out. The body is one straight line, ribs in, glutes tight, pushing the floor away through the shoulders the whole time.

Hollow hold, 2 × 20 seconds — on your back, lower back pressed into the floor, arms overhead, legs straight and lifted, one shallow banana. Then arch hold, 2 × 20 seconds — face down, arms and legs lifted. The two shapes every calisthenics skill is made of.

Planche leans, Tuesday and Thursday only, 3 × 15 seconds — a push-up position, then shift the shoulders forward past the wrists as far as they'll go, arms locked, body rigid. The home end of the slow lane: five seconds added per fortnight, never with an elbow that aches.

**Friday evening:** RANGE only. No skill block.

**If you skip it:** nothing shows this week. By week 6 the hips tighten back to where they were, the internal-rotation lift stops moving, and the tests say so in numbers. The weekly check asks how many evenings you did it.

## CALISTHENICS — THE LINES AND THE LEVELS

Seven lines, each with levels. **You own a level when you hit the top of its target for the prescribed sets on two occasions running. Then, and only then, move up.** Adding reps at a level always comes before adding difficulty. When you go back to a lower level, that's your warm-up.

**The rules that sit over all of it.** One to two reps in the tank on every set — the grinding lives on Saturday. Easy weeks (5 and 10): every line at half its sets, no slow lane. Taper weeks 15–16: holds only — handstand and ring support — nothing to failure, nothing new. Camp Mode: the handstand at home stays; everything else on this page stops. Weekly check: shoulders, elbows or wrists at 4 or above out of 10 on Sunday, and next week every line is holds only. The slow lane's three laws are absolute.

**THE SLOW LANE** — one lever hold on Sunday; planche leans at home Tuesday and Thursday

Three laws. **One:** add five seconds to a hold every two weeks, maximum. **Two:** twelve weeks minimum at each level, whatever the numbers say. **Three:** any ache on the inside of the elbow or the front of the shoulder, during or the next day, and the slow lane rests for two weeks — no exceptions, no negotiating. Straight-arm work loads the elbow tendons like nothing else, and tendon adapts over months while muscle adapts over weeks; for you, right now, that gap is wider than normal.

Front lever: scapular pulls 3 × 10 → tuck front lever 3 × 10 s → advanced tuck (back flat) → one leg → straddle → full. A two-to-four-year skill.
Back lever: skin the cat 3 × 3 → German hang 3 × 20 s → tuck back lever 3 × 10 s → one leg → straddle → full. Do not force the German hang; shoulders open over months.
Planche: leans 3 × 15 s → 3 × 30 s → feet-elevated leans → tuck planche on parallettes 3 × 5 s → advanced tuck → straddle. The longest road in calisthenics. The leans are most of the benefit for a fighter.

## TEST DAY — SATURDAY OF WEEK 16

Fresh after two easy weeks. These are your real numbers, and every percentage for the next 16 weeks comes off them. Most explosive first, full recovery between everything.

1. **Flying 20m sprint** — 3 attempts, best time. Alone: phone on a tripod side-on at the 20m zone, slow-mo, count frames between the two marks (at 240fps, 240 frames = 1 second). Or a cheap laser timing gate — the only piece of kit worth buying for this.
2. **Depth jump** — 3 attempts, best rebound height. Film it in slow-mo, take the flight time (feet off to feet on) in seconds: height in cm ≈ 123 × flight time². A 0.5-second flight is about 31 cm.
3. **Diagonal throw** — 3 per side, best distance.
4. **Shot-put throw** — 3 per side, best distance.
5. **Back squat** — true max single, pins set. Ramp as on the Saturday page.
6. **Bench press** — true max single, pins, no collars.
7. **Trap bar** — heavy 3RM (× 1.08 = new max).
8. **Tape and bodyweight** — arm, shoulders, waist.
9. **Sunday** — the 20-minute bike test in place of the fight rounds (Sunday page). Distance and peak heart rate.

Targets each 16 weeks: lifts +3–6% · throws and jump +3–5% · sprint 1–3% faster · 20-minute distance +3–5% · fight-sim fade 1–3 points better. If the fast-bar rule fired most weeks, the lifts will beat that range — the targets are floors, not ceilings. Then 3–4 easy days, and week 1 starts again off the new numbers.

## WHEN BOXING RETURNS — CAMP MODE

This program is a base phase: it assumes the evenings are free. The day your coach puts you back on the pads four or five nights a week — and more so the day a fight is booked — half of it becomes a liability, and a program that doesn't say which half isn't finished. This is the switch. It's one page because it has to be simple enough to flip in a week.

**What boxing takes over.** Technical sessions five nights a week are conditioning — intervals with a skill on top. So the two bike sessions and the fight simulation are the first things to go: boxing does their job better, and stacking them on top of it is how you arrive at sparring flat. The engine gets measured in the ring now, not on a bike.

**What boxing never covers.** Maximal strength, the power doses, the tendon block, the neck, the Nordics and the trunk. Those stay, and they get protected.

**THE CAMP WEEK — four sessions, about four hours:**

| Day | Session | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 3am · bench throw, box jumps, bench, chins, rows, band catch. Shoulder circuit at home. | ~48 min |
| Tuesday | SLEEP | — |
| Wednesday | 3am · sled, trap bar, pause squat, RDL, neck holds — the loads capped at 80% of the week table on any week with sparring | ~55 min |
| Thursday | 3am · throws + landmine, split squat, Spanish squat hold, Achilles hold, neck. No bike. | ~40 min |
| Friday | SLEEP | — |
| Saturday | The leg & power session, as written — sprints, drop jumps, side bounds, squat, jump circuit, push press. If Sunday is sparring, the jump circuit is cut to one round. | ~85 min |
| Sunday | Punch throws (2 rounds), trunk, Nordics, weekly check. No fight sim — the ring did it. | ~40 min |

RANGE and the morning five stay in camp — they're the cheapest injury insurance you own. Calisthenics in camp: the handstand at home stays; the rings, the pistols, the L-sits and the slow lane stop — boxing nights leave no room for a fourth pressing exposure or a tendon experiment.

Boxing nights: the head-contact rule is the law. The morning after any sparring is the LIGHT SESSION or nothing — no heavy lifts, no sprints, no jumps for 24 hours.

**The last ten days before a fight.** Week table numbers stop; every lift goes to 2 sets at 70%, fast; no sprints, no depth jumps, no Nordics inside the last seven days; the jump circuit becomes box jumps only. The two power doses stay because they keep the nervous system sharp without costing anything. The last heavy thing you do is nine days out. The fuel plan's "making weight" section runs the food.

**Coming back to base.** The week after a fight, or the week boxing drops back to two nights: this page closes, the full week reopens at an easy-week loading, and the count continues from wherever you were.

## THE THINKING (read at home, not in the gym)

**What this version is.** Optimal 8 Singles with the aesthetics taken out, the mobility taken home, the weekday sessions capped under an hour, the weekend sessions cut to what the fighter needs, shorter strength rests, and one new rule. Everything that makes you hit harder, move faster, last longer or stay in one piece is unchanged and at the same numbers.

**What changed in v1.1, and the coaching behind it.** Friday became sleep because the ride was the lowest-value session in the week and the alarm was the highest-cost one; a coach who lets a shift worker lose an hour of sleep for a token aerobic dose isn't coaching. The tendon block went in because the strength this program builds will outrun tendon adaptation — that's true for any fast-gaining lifter, and the sprints, depth jumps and Nordics are where the bill would land; two still holds a week, one Achilles, one patellar, is the cheapest insurance in sport. The mental drills that live inside sessions moved onto these pages because a drill you have to look up in a second document at heart rate 170 is a drill you don't do. And Camp Mode exists because the program was only ever the base phase of a boxer's year, and a boxer's program has to know what to do when the boxing comes back.

**Why the repeat bursts replaced the 30-second all-outs.** A boxer's hardest work is a flurry of six to ten seconds, twenty or thirty seconds apart, for three minutes. The 30-second all-out trained a quality the ring samples rarely, and it was the most expensive session in the week to recover from. The bursts train the exact shape of a round — and the ability to repeat a burst is decided by how fast the muscle refills between them, which is aerobic work done at flat-out intensity. The easy weekend hour is the base that refilling depends on, put where it costs no sleep.

**Why RANGE, and why it's twenty minutes.** You named your hips and shoulders as a limiting factor, and for a boxer they are — hip rotation is where the hook and the cross come from, shoulder range is what holds a guard without the traps. Eight minutes of mobility maintains; it never changes stiffness that's decades old. Change needs long holds with a long exhale, contract-relax to reset the guarding, strength at the end of the range so the body keeps it, and every day for three months. Twenty minutes is the smallest dose that does all four. The morning five keeps the joints honest between evenings. The get-ups and the crawls are the functional layer you asked for: the whole body agreeing on how to get off the floor, and the shoulder loaded in the one pattern nothing else in the week uses.

**Why the calisthenics is threaded, not stacked.** A standalone calisthenics session would have spent the one thing this week protects — an empty Friday before the biggest session — or created a double day on a work day. Threaded, each piece lands where it does most and costs least: pressing with the pressing, pulling with the pulling, the skill at home where frequency is free, the elbow-loading pieces spread across three days instead of spiking on one. Nothing was dropped to make room — the shoulder circuit, the rows, the split squat and the calf work all stay — because dropping proven work to fit new work is how programs get worse while looking busier. The weekday sessions now run to about an hour and a few minutes over; that is the right price, and it's the last minute this week will take.

**What went, and what it cost.** Three size blocks — 42 minutes a week — that never touched the fighter. Chest and triceps still get bench, bench throws and the weeks 11–14 clap circuit; shoulders and traps get push press, trap bar and chins; arms get chins and rows. Cost to the fighter: nothing. Cost to the look: some direct arm and shoulder volume, which the compound lifts cover well enough that the tape, not the mirror, will tell you.

**What moved, and why there.** The split squat left Saturday for Thursday: 24 hours after the heavy lower morning, 48 hours before Saturday's sprints, and before the bike so the reps stay clean. The seated calf raise left Saturday for the end of Tuesday: a low-cost lift in a low-cost slot. Mobility left every session for the evening — it does the same job at 8pm, and the eight minutes it freed are what keep four weekday sessions under an hour. The warm-ups did not move: they are what makes sprinting, jumping and lifting at 3am survivable, and they are not mobility.

**Why the rests are shorter.** Recovery is not the limit in this build; time is. Two minutes between strength sets is enough for triples and fives at these percentages, and 75–90 seconds is enough for rows and split squats. Speed, jumps, sprints and throws keep every second of their rest, because the moment those get tired they stop training what they're for.

**Why the fast-bar rule exists.** Percentages are a way of prescribing effort from a number, and the number is only as good as the day it was measured. When strength moves faster than the max weeks can catch it, every set between max weeks is lighter than intended, and the grind rule can only ever take weight off. The fast-bar rule is the other half: one question after the last set, one number moved 2.5%, once a week at most. It keeps the loading honest between measurements.

**Placement, unchanged.** The two sessions that decide this program — legs and power, and punches and rounds — sit on the weekend, fed, fresh, no shift after them. Wednesday is the heavy lower morning, 72 hours from Saturday. Friday is easy so Saturday isn't. The Nordics go last on Sunday with two leg-free days behind them. Saturday is 90 minutes now, not 120, and gives up nothing the fighter uses: sprints, reactive jumps, side bounds, heavy squat, the full jump circuit and the push press are all there, all fresh, in the same order.

**The evidence, unchanged.** Leg strength drives punch force (Loturco 2016; Dunn 2022). Throw speed drives punch speed. The engine is mostly aerobic, with brutal spikes — easy volume, 4-minute intervals (Helgerud 2007), 40-second repeats for the late-round burn, and the fight sim for the actual shape of a fight. Durability is cheap and targeted: neck, hands, Nordics, Copenhagen planks, cuff work.

**Stop the set when it slows.** Speed of movement is the quality being trained; grinding reps train grinding. Rep ranges are ceilings, not targets.

That's the science. The gym pages above are the program. From here, the numbers do the talking.
