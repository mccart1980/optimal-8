# OPTIMAL 8 · STRENGTH, POWER AND PHYSIQUE

**Twelve weeks to make you as strong, as explosive and as muscular as you can be, six days a week, with the base of your engine built underneath. It runs in three blocks — size, then strength, then power — and every block keeps all three going. It's its own programme, beside Prep: Settings → Fight booked? No → What runs → STRENGTH, POWER, PHYSIQUE. Prep doesn't change.**

Picked while a Prep is running, the Prep carries on to its test day and the twelve weeks start the Monday after. The weeks are S1 to S12 in the app; after S12 the cycle starts again from the new working maxes. Book a fight and the fight's plan takes over from the next Monday. The food is yours: the app runs the day as printed.

## THE WEEK

Six sessions: four heavy or fast, two for size with an easy run after, and an easy hour on the bike on Saturday evening. Friday is off. About ten and a quarter hours a week.

**Optimal 8 · Move runs beside it from Monday 16 November.** Every session opens with its movement block in the warm-up's place; Thursday's side plank and dead bug, and Saturday's side plank, become the Copenhagen plank; Sunday adds the back extension after the hammer curls; Monday's calf raise goes heavier and slower. Its evenings, its standards and every move's levels are in its own document, Optimal 8 · Move.

**Its tendon work goes in from Monday 23 November.** The lower pushes on Monday, Thursday and Saturday; the hip pair in the Copenhagen plank's place on Thursday and Saturday; Monday's calf raise on a towel; and at home, the upper pushes on Tuesday and Thursday evenings and in Session B. Every push and the reasons are in Optimal 8 · Move.

**The engine base from Monday 16 November.** Saturday evening gets the easy hour, 45 minutes on the bike, and Sunday's nasal run goes to 40 minutes. The lifting and the hard engine sessions don't change. Before that week, the two 30-minute runs held the engine where it was.

**The audit's changes from Monday 23 November.** Nothing comes out. Monday's movement block ends with three easy box jumps. Tuesday gets the knuckle hold, and the third minute of every pressure round is arms only. Spleeft reads every rep of the trap bar jumps, the bench throws and the landmine push press. The 4 × 4 is written for the treadmill, at 5%. Sunday gets a second dose of the medicine-ball drops. The throws use a 3 kg ball in the power block. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 test power: the rotational throw for distance, the push press speed test and the arms-only test. About four minutes a week more, and five or six more in each of those three weeks. The reasons are in the audit of 19 November.

**The core from Monday 23 November.** Every training day has at least three exercises for the abs and the core, and there's more rotation: Monday's box jumps become the kneeling jump into box jump, and Thursday gets a third day of throws. About 23 minutes a week more, 12 in the lighter weeks and test week. Every exercise and where it goes is in THE CORE, below.

## HOW THE WEIGHTS ARE SET

Every heavy weight comes off your working max, and bar speed decides the load. Everything else goes by how many reps you have left in you.

**Working max.** The same number the app keeps: your heaviest clean set of 3 with one or two reps left, × 1.08, rounded to 2.5 kg. Your current numbers carry straight over, and test week resets them.

**The ramp before every main lift — on the lift's own card.** Bar × 8, then 40% × 5, 55% × 3, 65% × 2, 75% × 1 and 85% × 1 — each step only while it's at least 5% under the day's working weight. A minute between.

**Bar speed.** Spleeft on your wrist, alert at a 20% slowdown, on the squat, bench and trap bar. When it beeps, that rep is the set's last: rack it, and the set counts. The first work set's speed — the mean of its two fastest reps — goes in the box under it, against the target the app reads off your profile at the day's percentage. More than 0.05 m/s slower: the remaining sets come down 5%. More than 0.05 m/s faster two sessions running: 2.5% goes on your working max from then on.

**Reps in reserve** — how many more clean reps you could have done:

- Main lifts: two or more. The beep usually stops you there.
- Presses, rows, dips, chins, split squats and Romanian deadlifts: one or two.
- Raises, curls, extensions, flyes, leg curls and calves: one or two, and the last set to failure or one short of it.

**Adding weight.** On anything with a rep range, when every set reaches the top of the range, add the smallest step next time. The main lifts rise with the table. Every exercise the app doesn't load for you has a weight box above its sets: type the weight, and it shows last time's weights and reps the next time round, tells you when every set hit the top of the range, and charts every lift in NUMBERS. Log the reps you actually did with ADJUST.

**Tempo.** Main lifts: down under control, up as fast as the bar will go. Everything else: about two seconds down to a full stretch, then up.

**Jumps and throws.** Every rep as fast, high or far as you can make it. A rep clearly slower or lower than the first ends the set. From Monday 23 November, Spleeft reads the trap bar jumps, the bench throws and the landmine push press too. Read the speed after every rep: a rep 10% slower than the set's fastest ends the set. The first set's speed, the mean of its two fastest reps, goes in the box under it, and PROGRESS charts it against other sessions at the same load.

**Peak-power loads.** Trap bar jumps and bench throws use the green row in THE PROFILE (MORE → SETTINGS). Weeks 4 and 8 redraw every profile, the jumps and throws included. From Monday 23 November, test week redraws the jumps' and throws' too.

**Pairs.** Two exercises on one card: a set of one, its rest, a set of the other. Every set is logged on its own exercise.

**No true maxes.** The heaviest you ever lift is a top triple, on pins.

## THE CORE — FROM MONDAY 23 NOVEMBER

**Every training day has at least three exercises for the abs and the core.** One for the abs themselves, with weight on it and taken close to failure, because the abs grow the way every other muscle does. One that braces the trunk or works the sides of the waist. One that turns, under load or fast. They come after the heavy lifting, never before it, so a tired trunk never has to hold a heavy squat or trap bar. Rotation goes up too: turning under load on four days, and the throws on three.

| Day | The three | New, and where it goes | Added |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Hanging leg raise · dead bug · cable woodchop, high to low | Dead bug + cable woodchop, a pair straight after the leg raises | ~5 min |
| Tuesday | Ab wheel rollout · landmine rotation · dumbbell side bend | Dumbbell side bend, after the ab wheel, before the knuckle hold | ~3 min |
| Wednesday | Cable crunch · medicine-ball drops · cable woodchop, low to high | Cable woodchop, low to high, after the side neck raise and the drops | ~4 min |
| Thursday | Rotational scoop throw · the star side plank in the hip pair · weighted decline sit-up | The scoop throw after the trap bar jumps; the sit-up at the end | ~7 min |
| Saturday | The star side plank in the hip pair · landmine rotation · hanging leg raise | Hanging leg raise, after the landmine rotation | ~4 min |
| Sunday | Cable crunch · medicine-ball drops · Pallof press | Nothing: it already has three | — |

About 23 minutes a week. The lighter weeks and test week run every one of them at half the sets, rounded up: about 12 minutes. Nothing changes before Monday 23 November.

**The new exercises**

1. **Kneeling jump into box jump** — Monday, in the box jumps' place: 4 × 2, all twelve weeks. Rest 60 seconds. Kneel tall on a mat, toes tucked under, a knee-high box about a metre in front of you. Swing your arms back, then throw them forward and snap your hips through to jump onto your feet, landing in a half squat. Straight away, jump onto the box as high as you can, land soft and step down. Kneel again and start each rep still. Each rep is two jumps, so the reps go from 3 to 2. From your knees the legs can't use a dip and a bounce, so all of the jump comes from snapping your hips through: the same drive that turns a punch from the back foot.
2. **Dead bug** — Monday, 2 × 8 each side, slow, back to back with the woodchop. Lie on your back, arms pointing at the ceiling, knees bent above your hips. Press your lower back into the floor and keep it there while you slowly lower one arm and the opposite leg to just above the floor, breathing out. Bring them back, then the other pair. When it's easy, hold a light plate in your hands.
3. **Cable woodchop, high to low** — Monday, 2 × 10 each side. Rest 45 seconds after each pair. A cable at the top of the stack with a single handle. Stand side-on to it, feet wide, both hands on the handle above your near shoulder, arms long. Pull it down and across your body to the outside of your far knee by turning your hips and shoulders together, your back foot pivoting, then let it back up under control. All the reps one side, then the other. Add weight when 10 is easy.
4. **Dumbbell side bend** — Tuesday, 2 × 12 each side. Rest 45 seconds. Stand tall with one heavy dumbbell in one hand, the other hand on your head. Bend straight sideways towards the dumbbell as far as you can, then pull back up past upright with the side of your waist. No twisting, no leaning forward. All the reps one side, then the other. Add weight when 15 is easy.
5. **Cable woodchop, low to high** — Wednesday, 3 × 10 each side. Rest 45 seconds. A cable at the bottom of the stack with a single handle. Stand side-on to it, feet wide, both hands on the handle outside your near knee, arms long. Pull it up and across your body to above your far shoulder by turning your hips and shoulders together, your back foot pivoting, then let it back down under control. All the reps one side, then the other. Add weight when 10 is easy.
6. **Rotational scoop throw** — Thursday, 3 × 3 each side, the 4–6 kg ball (the 3 kg ball in the power block), into a wall. Rest 45 seconds. Side-on to a solid wall, the ball held low at your back hip. Drive off your back foot, turn your hips through and throw it into the wall as hard as you can, arms long. Catch it and reset. All the throws one side, then the other. A throw clearly weaker than the first ends the set.
7. **Weighted decline sit-up** — Thursday, 3 × 10, at the end. Rest 45 seconds. On a decline bench, feet hooked under the pads, a plate held on your chest. Curl up one bit of your spine at a time until your chest faces your knees, then lower slowly all the way back. Add weight when 12 is easy.
8. **Hanging leg raise** — Saturday, 3 × 8–12. Rest 60 seconds. Monday's leg raise again: hang from the bar, curl your pelvis up and lift your knees to your chest without swinging, then lower slowly. Straight legs once that gets easy.

Every set is logged on its own exercise, with a weight box where it takes weight, and the app shows last time's.

## MONDAY · LOWER STRENGTH

Box jumps while you're fresh, then the lower pushes, then the squat heavy, then Romanian deadlifts, split squats and leg curls for size, then the neck and rear delts.

**The movement block · 8 minutes** — hips, knees and lower back

1. **Easy bike**, 2 minutes.
2. **90/90 hip switches**, 2 × 5 each way. **Cossack squats**, 1 × 5 each side, a 3-second pause at the bottom. **Bird dog**, 5 each side.
3. **Three easy box jumps**, at half effort, from Monday 23 November: the build-up before the hard sets on the next card.

Every move, its levels and the reasons are in Optimal 8 · Move. Before its week 1 this was the warm-up: the easy bike for 3 minutes and the Spanish squat hold, 2 × 30 seconds. Until Monday 23 November the block was 11 minutes, with the Spanish squat hold, 3 × 45 seconds; the knee pushes do its job harder.

**The session**

1. **Box jump** — 4 × 3, all twelve weeks. Rest 60 seconds. Jump onto a box about knee height, as high as you can, land soft and quiet, and step down. A jump clearly lower than the first ends the set. From Monday 23 November it's the kneeling jump into box jump, 4 × 2: THE CORE, above.
2. **The lower pushes** — about 10 minutes, from Monday 23 November. Every push: ease in over a second, push for 3 seconds, breathing out the whole time, relax for 3. Nothing moves: the push is the work. Four pushes make a set; one leg at a time, the legs in turn, five sets each. The first two weeks at 8 out of 10, then as hard as you can; a red morning at half effort; Achilles or knee at 3 or more on Sunday's check, 7 out of 10.
   - **Achilles pushes.** On the Smith machine, with the safety stops set just above the bar so it can't rise. Stand on one leg, the ball of that foot on the edge of a plate and the heel dropped a little below it, knee straight, the bar across your upper back and pressed up against the stops. Push the ball of your foot down, as if rising onto your toes.
   - **Knee pushes.** On the leg extension, one leg: the seat set so your knee lines up with the machine's pivot, the pad on the front of your ankle, the start position set so your knee is bent to a right angle, and the pin at the bottom of the stack. Push your shin forward into the pad.
   - In Optimal 8 · Move's test weeks the knee pushes become the single-leg leg extension, the heaviest you can lift for 5 clean reps each leg: one of the two tendon numbers.
3. **Back squat** — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. Rest 3 minutes. In the power block, do three box jumps 90 seconds after each double, then rest 2 minutes. Bar on your upper back, feet about shoulder width, toes slightly out. Brace, sit down between your heels until the crease of your hip is below the top of your knee, then drive up. Pins set just below your lowest point.
4. **Romanian deadlift** — size 3 × 8, strength 3 × 6, power 3 × 5. Rest 2 minutes. Barbell at arm's length, knees soft. Push your hips back and slide the bar down your thighs, back flat, until your hamstrings are at a deep stretch, then drive your hips forward to stand.
5. **Bulgarian split squat** — size 3 × 8 each leg, strength 3 × 6, power 2 × 6. Rest 90 seconds. Back foot on a bench behind you, a dumbbell in each hand, front foot far enough forward that its shin stays fairly upright. Lower until the back knee nearly touches the floor, then drive up through the front heel.
6. **Seated leg curl** — 3 × 10–12, all twelve weeks, the last set to failure. Rest 75 seconds. Knees lined up with the machine's pivot, the pad behind your legs just above your heels, the top pad down on your thighs, chest leaning slightly forward. Curl all the way, then lower slowly to a full stretch.
7. **Towel calf raise**, 3 × 6–10, back to back with 8. A rolled towel under your toes, so they're bent up as far as they go at the top: 3 seconds up, 2 at the top, 3 down, 2 at the bottom stretch, all the way down and all the way up. Heavy and slow for the Achilles, and the towel loads the sole of the foot too. Before Monday 23 November it was the standing calf raise without the towel or the pause at the top; before Optimal 8 · Move, 3 × 10–15. In Optimal 8 · Move's test weeks: the heaviest 5 clean reps, the other tendon number.
8. **Hanging leg raise**, 3 × 8–12. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. Hang from the bar, curl your pelvis up and lift your knees to your chest without swinging, then lower slowly. Straight legs once that gets easy.
9. **Lying neck curl**, 3 × 15–20, back to back with 10. Slow, never jerked. Lie face up on a bench with your head off the end, a folded towel and a light plate on your forehead, held with both hands. Let your head go back slowly, then curl your chin to your chest. Start with 2.5–5 kg and add a little only when 20 is easy.
10. **Lying neck extension**, 3 × 15–20. Rest 45 seconds after each pair. Lie face down on a bench with your head off the end, a folded towel and a light plate on the back of your head, held with both hands. Lower your chin towards your chest, then lift your head until you're looking straight ahead. Start light, as for the curl.
11. **Chest-supported rear-delt raise** — 3 × 15–20. Rest 60 seconds. Lie face down on an incline bench with light dumbbells hanging straight down. Raise your arms out to the sides to shoulder height, little fingers leading, squeeze the backs of your shoulders, then lower slowly.

## TUESDAY · UPPER STRENGTH

Rotational throws while you're fresh, then bench, weighted chins and the landmine press heavy, then landmine rotations, then rows and dips for size, the knuckle hold, then 6-second bursts and the pressure rounds on the assault bike.

**The movement block · 8 minutes** — lower back and shoulders

1. **Bird dog**, 5 each side.
2. **Cable external rotation at shoulder height**, 2 × 10–12 each arm, a 2-second hold.
3. **Push-up plus**, 10.
4. Two easy throws of each kind, at half effort.

**The session**

1. **Rotational shot-put throw**, alternated with 2 — 3 × 3 each side. Rest 45 seconds between each. A 4–6 kg medicine ball at your shoulder, side-on to a solid wall in your stance. Drive off your back hip and push the ball into the wall hard and flat, like a straight punch. Catch it and reset.
   - In the power block and test week, from Monday 23 November, a 3 kg ball for both throws.
   - In weeks 4, 8 and 12, from the same Monday, the **rotational throw for distance** comes first. Open floor, never towards a wall. The 4 kg ball at your shoulder, side-on in your stance. Drive off your back hip and throw it flat and as far as you can, and measure from your front foot to where it first lands. Three each side; log the best of the six.
2. **Hook throw** — 3 × 3 each side. Ball at chest height in bent arms, side-on to the wall. Pivot hard off your lead leg and sling the ball sideways into the wall.
3. **Bench press**, alternated with 4 — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. In the power block, do three medicine-ball chest passes 90 seconds after each double. Feet flat, backside on the bench, shoulder blades squeezed together. Lower to your lower chest, pause for a one-count, then press. Pins at chest height, no collars.
4. **Weighted chin-up, palms toward you** — size 4 × 6, strength 5 × 4, power 4 × 3, at the heaviest weight that leaves two in you. Palms facing you, hands shoulder width, weight on a belt. From a dead hang, pull until your chin clears the bar, then lower all the way. Go bench, 90 seconds, chins, 90 seconds, so each lift gets 3 minutes.
5. **One-arm landmine press** — size 3 × 8 each side, strength 4 × 5, power 3 × 4, at the heaviest weight that leaves two in you. Rest 2 minutes. One end of a barbell in a landmine or a corner, the other end at your shoulder in one hand. Stand in a split stance, the opposite foot forward, ribs down. Press the bar up and forward until your arm is straight, leaning into it, then lower it under control. Never lean back. Log the weight you add to the bar.
6. **Landmine rotation** — 3 × 6–8 each side, controlled. Rest 60 seconds. Straight after the landmine press, with the bar still in the landmine. The other end of the bar at arm's length in front of your chest, feet wide. Swing it down to one hip by turning your hips and pivoting your back foot, arms straight, then across to the other side, trunk braced. Start with the bar plus 5–10 kg; Saturday's is the same, so each day's weight starts from the other's.
7. **Chest-supported dumbbell row**, alternated with 8 — 3 × 8–10. Chest on a bench set at about 30 degrees, a dumbbell in each hand. Row your elbows back to your hips, squeeze, then lower to a full stretch.
8. **Weighted dip** — size 3 × 8–10, strength 3 × 6–8, power 3 × 5–6. Rest 90 seconds between each exercise. Parallel bars, weight on a belt once 10 bodyweight reps are easy. Lower until your shoulders are level with your elbows, chest slightly forward, then press to locked arms.
9. **Cable lateral raise** — 3 × 12–15. Rest 60 seconds. Cable at hand height, stand side-on, the handle in your far hand with the cable running behind your back. Raise your arm out to the side to shoulder height, leading with the elbow, then lower slowly. The cable keeps the shoulder working at the bottom, where a dumbbell goes slack.
10. **Ab wheel rollout** — 3 × 8–10. Rest 60 seconds. Kneeling, roll forward as far as you can without your lower back sagging, then pull back with your abs.
11. **Knuckle hold** — 3 × 20 seconds, from Monday 23 November; 2 × 20 in weeks 4, 8 and 12. Rest 30 seconds. A push-up position on your fists on a mat, wrists dead straight, forearm and knuckles in one line. Hold.
12. **Assault bike bursts** — 8 bursts, 4 in weeks 4, 8 and 12. On the assault bike, arms and legs together: 2 minutes easy, then each burst 6 seconds absolutely flat out with 40 seconds of easy spinning after it, then 2 minutes easy to finish. About 10 minutes. If a burst is clearly weaker than the one before, take an extra 20 seconds; if two in a row are, stop there.
13. **Pressure rounds** — 3 rounds, 2 in weeks 4, 8 and 12. Straight after the bursts, on the same bike: clear its screen, 1 minute easy, then 3-minute rounds with 1 minute's rest between. In each round, a 5-second burst all out every 20 seconds, and a steady pace between them, one you could hold for the round. Never stop pedalling. Then 2 minutes easy. About 15 minutes; the timer runs all of it. Off on a red morning.
    - From Monday 23 November, **the third minute of every round is arms only**: feet on the pegs on the bike's fork, legs still, your arms driving the handles through the same bursts and the same steady pace. The timer says when to change. Log the minutes at 90% or above too: the time in the Polar's top heart-rate zone for the whole session.
    - From the same Monday, weeks 4, 8 and 12 run one round, then **the arms-only test** in the second round's place: a minute easy, clear the screen, feet on the pegs, then 3 minutes on your arms alone for the biggest total you can make, and 2 minutes easy. Log the total and your peak heart rate.

## WEDNESDAY · PHYSIQUE A

Chest and back for size, rear delts and cable crunches, the neck and medicine-ball drops, then a 30-minute nasal run. Nothing heavy and nothing fast, because Thursday is the power session.

**The movement block · 9 minutes** — hips, ankles and overhead

1. **Goblet squat pry**, 2 × 30 seconds. **Prone Y-raise**, 2 × 10. **Side-lying dumbbell external rotation**, 2 × 12 each arm.
2. **Push-up plus**, 10, then a light set of exercises 1 and 2.

**The session**

1. **Incline dumbbell press**, alternated with 2 — 3 × 8–12. Bench at 30 degrees. Lower the dumbbells to the sides of your upper chest at a deep stretch, then press up and slightly in.
2. **Lat pulldown** — 3 × 10–12. Rest 90 seconds between each exercise. Lean back slightly and pull the bar to your upper chest, driving your elbows down, then let it all the way up to a full stretch.
3. **Low-to-high cable fly**, alternated with 4 — 3 × 12–15. Cables set low, step forward, and sweep your hands up and together to chin height, arms slightly bent. Lower to a full stretch.
4. **Wide-grip seated cable row** — 3 × 10–12. Rest 75 seconds between each exercise. Pull the bar to your upper chest with your elbows out, squeeze your upper back, then reach forward to a full stretch.
5. **Reverse pec-deck**, 3 × 15–20, back to back with 6. Face the machine, arms straight out in front, and sweep them back until they're level with your body.
6. **Cable crunch**, 3 × 10–15. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. Kneel facing a high cable, the rope held either side of your head. Curl your ribs down towards your hips, so your back rounds and your hips stay still, then uncurl slowly. Add weight when 15 is easy.
7. **Dumbbell lateral raise** — 4 × 12–20. Rest 60 seconds. Lean slightly forward, raise the dumbbells out to the sides to shoulder height, elbows soft, and lower slowly. On the last set, when you reach failure, pick up dumbbells about 30% lighter and go again.
8. **Side neck raise**, 3 × 12–15 each side, back to back with 9. Lie on your side on a bench with your head off the end. Lower your ear slowly towards the floor, then lift it towards the ceiling. No weight to start; when 15 is easy, hold a light plate against the side of your head. Never jerk it.
9. **Medicine-ball drops**, 3 × 8. Rest 45 seconds after each pair. Lie on your back, knees bent, a 3 kg ball held at arm's length over your stomach. Breathe out hard through your teeth and brace as you let it go; it lands just above your belly button, and you catch it on the bounce. Drops 5 to 8 land a hand's width to each side. Never on the ribs. Go up a kilo when all eight land on a brace that doesn't give, up to 6 kg.

**Then · the nasal run, 30 minutes**

Easy, on the road or a treadmill, breathing in and out through your nose the whole way. If you need your mouth, slow down.

## THURSDAY · LOWER POWER

Sled, sprints and jumps while you're fresh, then the lower pushes, then the trap bar heavy, heavy shrugs on the same bar and a second, lighter squat, then the hip pair. Friday's rest after it.

**The movement block · 12 minutes** — hips, balance and sprint prep

1. **Easy bike**, 2 minutes.
2. **Leg swings**, 10 each way on each leg. **Hip airplanes**, 2 × 4 each side. **World's greatest stretch**, 3 each side. **A-skips**, 2 × 15 metres: skip forward, driving one knee up to hip height while the other foot paws the ground.
3. **Sprint build-ups**: three runs of 20 metres, at 60%, 75% and 90%.

**The session**

1. **Heavy sled sprint** — 5 × 20 metres. Rest 2 minutes. Load 40–60% of your bodyweight on the sled. Lean in at about 45 degrees and drive the ground backward through the whole foot, 5–7 seconds a run. Faster than that, add weight; slower, take some off.
2. **Flying sprint** — 3 runs in the size block, 4 in the strength and power blocks. Weeks 1 and 2 at 90%, flat out from week 3. Rest 2½ minutes. Jog-build for 15 metres, then run 20 metres absolutely flat out, and walk back. A hamstring twinge on the build-ups ends the sprints for the day.
3. **Jumps** — 4 × 3. Rest 90 seconds. Size block: box jumps, onto a box about knee height, landing soft, stepping down. Strength block: low-box depth jumps — step off a 20 cm box and, the instant your feet touch, jump straight up as high as you can. Power block: depth jumps, the same off a 30–40 cm box.
4. **Trap bar jump** — 4 × 3 at your peak-power load. Rest 90 seconds. Stand inside the bar, dip and jump as high as you can, land soft with bent knees, reset, go again. From Monday 23 November, Spleeft on your wrist: read the speed after every rep, and a rep 10% slower than the set's fastest ends the set.
5. **The lower pushes** — the Achilles pushes and the knee pushes, 5 × 4 each leg, as on Monday.
6. **Trap bar deadlift** — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. Rest 3 minutes. In the power block, do three broad jumps 90 seconds after each double: two-foot jump forward for distance, stick the landing. ## THE ENGINE

The base is built and the hard work is held. The two nasal runs and the Saturday easy hour are about two hours a week of easy work: they build the base of your engine, the part that builds slowly and lasts. The 4 × 4 holds the top of it, and Tuesday's bursts and pressure rounds add a little power, repeat-effort fitness and fight pace on top. Intensity is what holds the top, so the hard session has to stay genuinely hard.

There's no third hard session, and no hard engine work in the 24 hours before Monday or Thursday. Every free slot would put a third one within a day of Monday's squat or Thursday's sprints and jumps, which is where cardio costs power. It's also the part of the engine that fades within two or three weeks, so the camp brings it, closer to the fight.

**The nasal runs · Wednesday 30 minutes, Sunday 40**

- Breathe in and out through your nose the whole way. If you need your mouth, slow down.
- Heart rate around 65–75% of your peak: the easy zone the app gives you. Flat ground or a treadmill.
- Never on Monday, Thursday or Saturday. The runs stay off the heavy and fast days.
- Sunday's is 30 minutes in the lighter weeks and test week.
- Log the distance and your average heart rate.

**The Saturday easy hour · 45 minutes, in the evening**

- On a bike, at least six hours after the morning's session. The bike spares your Achilles and knees; it isn't that it costs the lifting less than running would.
- Nose only, at 65–75% of your peak heart rate, the same easy zone as the runs. If you need your mouth, slow down.
- 30 minutes in the lighter weeks and test week.
- Log your average heart rate. The same ride at a lower heart rate is the base growing.
- The sauna, if you use one: switch it on in FOOD on the day and it comes straight after, 15–20 minutes, with its water. Never straight into cold.

**The 4 × 4 · Saturday, after the session**

1. 5 minutes easy on the treadmill, set to 5%.
2. Four efforts of 4 minutes at 90–95% of your peak heart rate. The first minute climbs into that band; the last three sit in it.
3. 3 minutes easy between efforts, walking or jogging on the same slope, at about 70%.
4. 3 minutes easy to finish.

Use the same treadmill and the same slope every week. Log the distance on its screen at the end and your peak heart rate from the Polar. Progress is more distance at the same peak heart rate, or the same at a lower one. In the lighter weeks it's 3 × 4, read only against other 3 × 4s, and in test week the 4 × 4 is the engine test. Before Monday 23 November the card was written for the bike, and logged the total on its screen.

**The bursts · Tuesday, after the session**

Eight 6-second bursts on the assault bike, 40 seconds easy spinning between, 2 minutes easy before and after: about 10 minutes, and the timer runs all of it. Log the total on the screen at the end and your peak heart rate. Four in the lighter weeks and test week, read only against other fours.

**The pressure rounds · Tuesday, straight after the bursts**

Three rounds of 3 minutes on the same assault bike, with 1 minute's rest between. In each round, a 5-second burst all out every 20 seconds, nine a round, and a steady pace between them, never stopping. A minute easy before and 2 minutes easy after: about 15 minutes, and the timer runs all of it. Clear the bike's screen after the bursts, then log its total at the end and your peak heart rate. Two rounds in the lighter weeks and test week, read only against other twos.

From Monday 23 November the third minute of every round is arms only: feet on the pegs, legs still, your arms driving the handles through the same bursts and the same steady pace. Every other hard minute of the week is driven by your legs; this is the one your arms carry. In weeks 4, 8 and 12 it's one round, then the arms-only test: clear the screen, feet on the pegs, 3 minutes on your arms alone for the biggest total you can make, then 2 minutes easy. One round is read only against other single rounds, and the test only against the test before it.

Log one more number with the rounds: the minutes at 90% of your peak or above, which is the time in the Polar's top heart-rate zone for the session. Set the Polar's maximum heart rate to your peak once, so its top zone is the right one. With Saturday's 16, it's the week's dose of hard engine work.

They're the fight sim: bursts with seconds to recover, the way you fight. They cost the power work a little, so they're watched. If Thursday's sled times get slower at the same load, or your jump and throw sets keep ending early, two weeks running, the Saturday easy hour comes out first. If it happens again, two more weeks running, the pressure rounds come out too, for the rest of the twelve weeks.

## THE RULES

These protect the work, and none of them is optional.

1. **Lighter weeks, 4 and 8.** The squat, bench and trap bar are replaced by their profiles in THE PROFILE: five loads, two fast reps each. Trap bar jumps and bench throws get their four-load profiles. Everything else, the jumps, sled and sprints included, is half the sets, rounded up, the 4 × 4 becomes 3 × 4, the bursts become four, and the pressure rounds two; from Monday 23 November, one round and then the arms-only test.
2. **The morning check.** Yellow: every load on the bar 7% lighter, and the 4 × 4 at 90%. Red: the heavy lifts, the sprints, jumps and throws, the 4 × 4, the bursts and the pressure rounds come off; the size work runs as written, and so do the nasal runs and the easy hour; the pushes run at half effort, that evening's too. Three red mornings in a week make the next week a lighter week.
3. **Pain.** Anything over 3 out of 10 while you do it, or worse the next morning, stops that exercise for the day; the jumps, sprints and holds have the button for it. Sunday's check runs the tendon gate: Achilles or knee at 3 or more means no reactive jumps the next week, the lower pushes carried on at 7 out of 10, and once it's back under 3 they restart one stage down for a week; a hamstring at 3 or more means no flying sprints and the Nordics only if they're pain-free; an elbow or a shoulder at 4 or more turns the next week's dips into push-ups and takes Sunday's curls and triceps work off. The same pain two weeks running means a physio.
4. **Sleep.** Friday is off. Aim for seven hours a night, which at a 3 am start means asleep by 8 pm.
5. **Boxing evenings.** If you're still boxing, it's extra load on top of all this. When it tips you into red mornings, the Saturday easy hour goes first, then the nasal runs, because the boxing covers that work.
6. **No true maxes.** Top triples on pins, never singles.
7. **Bloods and blood pressure** before each cycle: full blood count, lipids, liver, kidneys and blood pressure. With what you're running, those are the numbers that matter.

**What counts as progress**

- Higher working maxes on the squat, bench and trap bar.
- A longer broad jump, and heavier peak-power loads in the profiles.
- A longer rotational throw, a faster push press at the same weight, and a bigger arms-only total.
- Arms and shoulders up on the tape, with the waist the same or smaller.
- More distance in the 4 × 4, at the same peak heart rate.

**What to expect, honestly**

- The first cycle moves fastest, because what time off took comes back quicker than it was built.
- After that, at 45 and already trained, the big lifts move a few per cent a cycle, not tens. That's a good result, not a failure.
- Power is the hardest quality to raise at your age, and it fades fastest. That's why the fast work runs in every block, never just the last one.
- The engine: a deeper base by test week, a few per cent, not a transformation, shown as a lower heart rate on the same easy work. The camp builds the fight-specific engine on top of it.
- The programme gives the stimulus; food and sleep make the look. Short nights will cost you more than any set or rep in here.

## WHY IT'S BUILT THIS WAY

Every choice below rests on trials where trials exist. Where it rests on coaching practice instead, it says so.

- **Planned variation, all three qualities every week.** Periodised training built more strength than unvaried training, and programmes that varied the load within the week did best (Williams, 2017). Hence the size, strength and power blocks, with every lift heavy on one day and lighter or faster on another.
- **Each lift twice a week, 10–20 hard sets per muscle.** Strength rises with how often a lift is trained. Strength and size both rise with weekly sets, but the returns flatten sooner for strength (Pelland, 2026). So the heavy lifts stay low in sets, and the extra sets go to size, with shoulders, chest, back and arms getting the most.
- **Bar speed ends the heavy sets.** Stopping squat sets at a 20% slowdown built the same strength as grinding on to 40%, and raised the jump 9.5% against 3.5%, with 40% fewer reps. Grinding built more thigh muscle, which is why the size work goes closer to failure and the heavy work doesn't (Pareja-Blanco, 2017).
- **Three minutes between heavy sets.** In trained men, 3 minutes' rest built more strength and size than 1 minute: the squat rose 15.2% against 7.6% (Schoenfeld, 2016).
- **Power built on strength, by several methods at once.** Power needs strength first, then throws, loaded jumps and rebound jumps on top (Cormie, 2011). The profile sets your loads and nothing more: a trial found that tailoring the work to a force and speed profile did no better than not tailoring it (Lindberg, 2021). Loaded jumps and throws stand in for cleans and snatches: the same intent to move fast, without years of technique or the wrist risk. That last part is a coaching call, not a trial.
- **A speed on every fast rep.** Athletes shown their speed after every rep moved the bar 8.4% faster on the spot, and gained more jump and sprint over weeks of training (Weakley, 2023). Hence Spleeft on the trap bar jumps, the bench throws and the landmine push press, and a 10% drop ending the set.
- **The sled, five runs.** Heavy sled sprints improve the first metres of a sprint and the forward push (Alcaraz, 2018; Morin, 2017). A sled run has no landing, so it's the power work that costs the joints least, and five runs is still well under the ten, twice a week, that Morin's trial used.
- **Two jump days.** Thursday carries the main jumps; Monday's box jumps, before the squat, make a second day. Box jumps land the softest of all the jumps, and a few fast jumps sharpen the heavy sets that follow. Two short jump days is coaching practice, and the Sunday tendon check still governs the rebound jumps.
- **Rotation on most days, and a trunk that holds.** The legs make a punch's force; the trunk turns it and carries it to the fist. Tuesday's shot-put and hook throws and Saturday's scoop throw train that turn fast, the straight and the hook, and from Monday 23 November Thursday's scoop throw makes it three days; the landmine rotation, Tuesday and Saturday, trains it under load; from the same Monday so do the cable woodchops, Monday and Wednesday; and the Pallof press trains the trunk to hold against a twist. That rests on how the punch works and on coaching practice, not a trial.
- **Abs, traps, rear delts and the neck.** Abs grow like any other muscle, on load and hard sets: 12 a week from the leg raises, the ab wheel and the cable crunches, which take weight, and from Monday 23 November 18, with Thursday's decline sit-ups and Saturday's leg raises, plus the dead bug, the side bend and the woodchops: three exercises for the core every training day. On top, Thursday and Saturday finish the lifting with trunk work that's never taken to failure: the hip pair on Thursday, and the hip pair then landmine rotations on Saturday. The traps get heavy shrugs twice a week, the rear delts direct work three times and the neck its own work twice. That's a coaching call for the build you're after, not a trial.
- **Pressing up and forward, not straight overhead.** A bar can only finish straight overhead if the shoulders reach all the way up. When they're stiff, the lower back arches to make up the difference, with the bar's weight on it. The landmine's bar travels up and forward, so both presses finish in front of you. The barbell push press produced as much power as a jump squat (Lake, 2014), and one arm moves less weight, so some leg power is given up; Thursday's jumps, sled and sprints cover it. The swap is a coaching call from how the lifts work, not a trial.
- **A neck trained in every direction, and a brace that fires on time.** Monday trains the neck forward and back, Wednesday side to side. In pooled trials, neck training with a load made the neck stronger bending forward and to the side (Ivanic, 2024). No trial yet shows that a stronger neck prevents concussion, but a 2025 expert consensus sets out how to train it for the knocks the head takes in sport (Fownes-Walpole, 2025). Wednesday's medicine-ball drops train the trunk to brace the instant something lands on it. No study backs the drops; they're long-standing coaching practice.
- **Exercises chosen for the stretch.** Overhead triceps extensions grew the triceps 1.4 times as much as pushdowns (Maeo, 2023). Seated leg curls grew the hamstrings more than lying ones, 14% against 9% (Maeo, 2021). The incline curls, deep presses and full-stretch raises follow the same idea.
- **The base built, the hard work held.** Aerobic endurance and maximum strength both build slowly and both last about a month once built, so block periodisation builds them together, early; the fight-specific engine fades within two or three weeks, so it's built in the camp (Issurin, 2008). Cardio barely holds back strength or muscle size, but it does blunt explosive power, mostly in the same session; three or more hours apart, the cost wasn't significant (Schumann, 2022). In trained lifters the cost showed when the two were within 20 minutes of each other, not when they were more than two hours apart (Petré, 2021). Men lose more lower-body strength and power to it than women (Huiberts, 2024), and 24 hours between them suited strength and power best (Robineau, 2016). So the easy work is easy and hours from the lifting, and the hard work is two sessions on the upper-body days, two days clear of Thursday's sprints and jumps. An older review found running cost more than cycling (Wilson, 2012); newer ones don't confirm it (Schumann, 2022; Sabag, 2018), so the bike is for the joints, not the interference. Endurance can be held for up to 15 weeks on as little as two hard sessions a week, as long as the intensity stays (Spiering, 2021). The 4 × 4 at 90–95% of peak heart rate raised fitness more than steady or threshold work (Helgerud, 2007), and people in their 40s gained as much from it as people in their 20s, about 11% in eight weeks at three a week (Støren, 2017). A bigger base also speeds recovery between hard efforts (Tomlin and Wenger, 2001): between bursts, and between rounds.
- **The sauna after the easy hour.** In six runners, three weeks of it after training raised the time they could run to exhaustion by 32% and their blood plasma by 7% (Scoon, 2007); in 20 runners, fitness rose 8% against 2% without it (Kirby, 2021). The studies are small and the proof is weak, but it takes nothing from the lifting.
- **The pressure rounds: three, not six.** Even a 3 × 2 fight is mostly aerobic, about 77% of its energy (Davis, 2014), but it's fought in short bursts with seconds between them: elite boxers work at about 18 to 1 activity to rest, novices at 9 to 1 (Slimani, 2017). The pressure rounds copy that on the bike. What conditioning costs power and size grows with how often and how long it is (Wilson, 2012), so it's three rounds, on one day. The full fight sim comes in the camp.
- **The arms-only minute.** Every other hard minute of the week is driven by the legs, and fitness built in one set of muscles only partly carries over to another: after 12 weeks of arm or leg training, gains were about 19–22% in the limbs that trained and 8–9% in the others (Pogliaghi, 2006). In boxers, four weeks of short all-out punching intervals raised the arms' peak oxygen uptake and punch force (Kamandulis, 2018). The evidence is thin, and the minute costs no time.
- **The 4 × 4 uphill on a treadmill.** It's how the trial behind it was run, at about 5% (Helgerud, 2007), and it's the machine you use.
- **The knuckle hold and the second dose of drops.** In Great Britain's boxing squad the hand was injured more than any other part of the body, in training and in competition (Loosemore, 2015). Both are coaching calls: no trial has tested either.
- **Hard days and easy days.** Heavy and fast sessions alternate with size work and easy running, and upper and lower body each get a heavy day and a fast day, as the conjugate method splits them. That shape comes from coaching practice — Charlie Francis's sprinters, Westside's lifters — more than from trials.
