# OPTIMAL 8 · STRENGTH, POWER AND PHYSIQUE

**Twelve weeks to make you as strong, as explosive and as muscular as you can be, six days a week, with your engine held where it is. It runs in three blocks — size, then strength, then power — and every block keeps all three going. It's its own programme, beside Prep and Optimal 8 Fighter: Settings → Fight booked? No → What runs → STRENGTH, POWER, PHYSIQUE. Neither of them changes.**

Picked while a Prep is running, the Prep carries on to its test day and the twelve weeks start the Monday after. The weeks are S1 to S12 in the app; after S12 the cycle starts again from the new working maxes. Book a fight and the fight's plan takes over from the next Monday. The food is yours: the app runs the day as printed.

## THE WEEK

Six sessions: four heavy or fast, two for size with an easy run after. Friday is off. About eight and three-quarter hours a week.

**Optimal 8 · Move runs beside it from Monday 16 November.** Every session opens with its movement block in the warm-up's place; Thursday's side plank and dead bug become the Copenhagen plank; Sunday adds the back extension after the hammer curls; Monday's calf raise goes heavier and slower. Its evenings, its standards and every move's levels are in its own document, Optimal 8 · Move.

Heavy and fast days alternate with size days, and the easy runs sit on the size days. That keeps the runs and the fatigue away from the sessions where speed and heavy weights matter most.

## HOW THE WEIGHTS ARE SET

Every heavy weight comes off your working max, and bar speed decides the load. Everything else goes by how many reps you have left in you.

**Working max.** The same number the app keeps: your heaviest clean set of 3 with one or two reps left, × 1.08, rounded to 2.5 kg. Your current numbers carry straight over, and test week resets them.

**The ramp before every main lift — on the lift's own card.** Bar × 8, then 40% × 5, 55% × 3, 65% × 2, 75% × 1 and 85% × 1 — each step only while it's at least 5% under the day's working weight. A minute between.

**Bar speed.** Spleeft on your wrist, alert at a 20% slowdown, on the squat, bench and trap bar. When it beeps, that rep is the set's last: rack it, and the set counts. The first work set's speed — the mean of its two fastest reps — goes in the box under it, against the target the app reads off your profile at the day's percentage. More than 0.05 m/s slower: the remaining sets come down 5%. More than 0.05 m/s faster two sessions running: 2.5% goes on your working max from then on.

**Reps in reserve** — how many more clean reps you could have done:

- Main lifts: two or more. The beep usually stops you there.
- Presses, rows, dips, chins, split squats and Romanian deadlifts: one or two.
- Raises, curls, extensions, flyes, leg curls and calves: one or two, and the last set to failure or one short of it.

**Adding weight.** On anything with a rep range, when every set reaches the top of the range, add the smallest step next time. The main lifts rise with the table. Every exercise the app doesn't load for you has a weight box above its sets: type the weight, and it shows last time's weights and reps the next time round, tells you when every set hit the top of the range, and charts every lift in NUMBERS. Log the reps you actually did with ADJUST.

**Tempo.** Main lifts: down under control, up as fast as the bar will go. Everything else: about two seconds down to a full stretch, then up.

**Jumps and throws.** Every rep as fast, high or far as you can make it. A rep clearly slower or lower than the first ends the set.

**Peak-power loads.** Trap bar jumps and bench throws use the green row in THE PROFILE (MORE → SETTINGS). Weeks 4 and 8 redraw every profile, the jumps and throws included.

**Pairs.** Two exercises on one card: a set of one, its rest, a set of the other. Every set is logged on its own exercise.

**No true maxes.** The heaviest you ever lift is a top triple, on pins.

## MONDAY · LOWER STRENGTH

Box jumps while you're fresh, then the squat heavy, then Romanian deadlifts, split squats and leg curls for size, then the neck and rear delts.

**The movement block · 11 minutes** — hips, knees and lower back

1. **Easy bike**, 2 minutes.
2. **90/90 hip switches**, 2 × 5 each way. **Cossack squats**, 1 × 5 each side, a 3-second pause at the bottom. **Bird dog**, 5 each side.
3. **Spanish squat hold**, 3 × 45 seconds, hard, 30 seconds between. A heavy band looped round a rack at knee height and behind your knees; sit back to a quarter squat, shins vertical, and hold. For the knee tendons.

Every move, its levels and the reasons are in Optimal 8 · Move. Before its week 1 this was the warm-up: the easy bike for 3 minutes and the Spanish squat hold, 2 × 30 seconds.

**The session**

1. **Box jump** — 4 × 3, all twelve weeks. Rest 60 seconds. Jump onto a box about knee height, as high as you can, land soft and quiet, and step down. A jump clearly lower than the first ends the set.
2. **Back squat** — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. Rest 3 minutes. In the power block, do three box jumps 90 seconds after each double, then rest 2 minutes. Bar on your upper back, feet about shoulder width, toes slightly out. Brace, sit down between your heels until the crease of your hip is below the top of your knee, then drive up. Pins set just below your lowest point.
3. **Romanian deadlift** — size 3 × 8, strength 3 × 6, power 3 × 5. Rest 2 minutes. Barbell at arm's length, knees soft. Push your hips back and slide the bar down your thighs, back flat, until your hamstrings are at a deep stretch, then drive your hips forward to stand.
4. **Bulgarian split squat** — size 3 × 8 each leg, strength 3 × 6, power 2 × 6. Rest 90 seconds. Back foot on a bench behind you, a dumbbell in each hand, front foot far enough forward that its shin stays fairly upright. Lower until the back knee nearly touches the floor, then drive up through the front heel.
5. **Seated leg curl** — 3 × 10–12, all twelve weeks, the last set to failure. Rest 75 seconds. Knees lined up with the machine's pivot, the pad behind your legs just above your heels, the top pad down on your thighs, chest leaning slightly forward. Curl all the way, then lower slowly to a full stretch.
6. **Standing calf raise**, 3 × 6–10, back to back with 7: 3 seconds up, 3 seconds down, 2 seconds at the bottom. All the way down to a full stretch, then all the way up: heavier and slower for the Achilles (before Optimal 8 · Move, 3 × 10–15).
7. **Hanging leg raise**, 3 × 8–12. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. Hang from the bar, curl your pelvis up and lift your knees to your chest without swinging, then lower slowly. Straight legs once that gets easy.
8. **Lying neck curl**, 3 × 15–20, back to back with 9. Slow, never jerked. Lie face up on a bench with your head off the end, a folded towel and a light plate on your forehead, held with both hands. Let your head go back slowly, then curl your chin to your chest. Start with 2.5–5 kg and add a little only when 20 is easy.
9. **Lying neck extension**, 3 × 15–20. Rest 45 seconds after each pair. Lie face down on a bench with your head off the end, a folded towel and a light plate on the back of your head, held with both hands. Lower your chin towards your chest, then lift your head until you're looking straight ahead. Start light, as for the curl.
10. **Chest-supported rear-delt raise** — 3 × 15–20. Rest 60 seconds. Lie face down on an incline bench with light dumbbells hanging straight down. Raise your arms out to the sides to shoulder height, little fingers leading, squeeze the backs of your shoulders, then lower slowly.

## TUESDAY · UPPER STRENGTH

Rotational throws while you're fresh, then bench, weighted chins and the landmine press heavy, then landmine rotations, then rows and dips for size, then 6-second bursts and the pressure rounds on the assault bike.

**The movement block · 8 minutes** — lower back and shoulders

1. **Bird dog**, 5 each side.
2. **Cable external rotation at shoulder height**, 2 × 10–12 each arm, a 2-second hold.
3. **Push-up plus**, 10.
4. Two easy throws of each kind, at half effort.

**The session**

1. **Rotational shot-put throw**, alternated with 2 — 3 × 3 each side. Rest 45 seconds between each. A 4–6 kg medicine ball at your shoulder, side-on to a solid wall in your stance. Drive off your back hip and push the ball into the wall hard and flat, like a straight punch. Catch it and reset.
2. **Hook throw** — 3 × 3 each side. Ball at chest height in bent arms, side-on to the wall. Pivot hard off your lead leg and sling the ball sideways into the wall.
3. **Bench press**, alternated with 4 — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. In the power block, do three medicine-ball chest passes 90 seconds after each double. Feet flat, backside on the bench, shoulder blades squeezed together. Lower to your lower chest, pause for a one-count, then press. Pins at chest height, no collars.
4. **Weighted chin-up, palms toward you** — size 4 × 6, strength 5 × 4, power 4 × 3, at the heaviest weight that leaves two in you. Palms facing you, hands shoulder width, weight on a belt. From a dead hang, pull until your chin clears the bar, then lower all the way. Go bench, 90 seconds, chins, 90 seconds, so each lift gets 3 minutes.
5. **One-arm landmine press** — size 3 × 8 each side, strength 4 × 5, power 3 × 4, at the heaviest weight that leaves two in you. Rest 2 minutes. One end of a barbell in a landmine or a corner, the other end at your shoulder in one hand. Stand in a split stance, the opposite foot forward, ribs down. Press the bar up and forward until your arm is straight, leaning into it, then lower it under control. Never lean back. Log the weight you add to the bar.
6. **Landmine rotation** — 3 × 6–8 each side, controlled. Rest 60 seconds. Straight after the landmine press, with the bar still in the landmine. The other end of the bar at arm's length in front of your chest, feet wide. Swing it down to one hip by turning your hips and pivoting your back foot, arms straight, then across to the other side, trunk braced. Start with the bar plus 5–10 kg; Saturday's is the same, so each day's weight starts from the other's.
7. **Chest-supported dumbbell row**, alternated with 8 — 3 × 8–10. Chest on a bench set at about 30 degrees, a dumbbell in each hand. Row your elbows back to your hips, squeeze, then lower to a full stretch.
8. **Weighted dip** — size 3 × 8–10, strength 3 × 6–8, power 3 × 5–6. Rest 90 seconds between each exercise. Parallel bars, weight on a belt once 10 bodyweight reps are easy. Lower until your shoulders are level with your elbows, chest slightly forward, then press to locked arms.
9. **Cable lateral raise** — 3 × 12–15. Rest 60 seconds. Cable at hand height, stand side-on, the handle in your far hand with the cable running behind your back. Raise your arm out to the side to shoulder height, leading with the elbow, then lower slowly. The cable keeps the shoulder working at the bottom, where a dumbbell goes slack.
10. **Ab wheel rollout** — 3 × 8–10. Rest 60 seconds. Kneeling, roll forward as far as you can without your lower back sagging, then pull back with your abs.
11. **Assault bike bursts** — 8 bursts, 4 in weeks 4, 8 and 12. On the assault bike, arms and legs together: 2 minutes easy, then each burst 6 seconds absolutely flat out with 40 seconds of easy spinning after it, then 2 minutes easy to finish. About 10 minutes. If a burst is clearly weaker than the one before, take an extra 20 seconds; if two in a row are, stop there.
12. **Pressure rounds** — 3 rounds, 2 in weeks 4, 8 and 12. Straight after the bursts, on the same bike: clear its screen, 1 minute easy, then 3-minute rounds with 1 minute's rest between. In each round, a 5-second burst all out every 20 seconds, and a steady pace between them, one you could hold for the round. Never stop pedalling. Then 2 minutes easy. About 15 minutes; the timer runs all of it. Off on a red morning.

## WEDNESDAY · PHYSIQUE A

Chest and back for size, rear delts and cable crunches, the neck and medicine-ball drops, then a 30-minute nasal run. Nothing heavy and nothing fast, because Thursday is the power session.

**The movement block · 9 minutes** — hips, ankles and overhead

1. **Goblet squat pry**, 2 × 30 seconds. **Prone Y-raise**, 2 × 10. **Side-lying dumbbell external rotation**, 2 × 12 each arm.
2. **Push-up plus**, 10, then a light set of exercises 1 and 2.

**The session**

1. **Incline dumbbell press**, alternated with 2 — 3 × 8–12. Bench at 30 degrees. Lower the dumbbells to the sides of your upper chest at a deep stretch, then press up and slightly in.
2. **Lat pulldown** — 3 × 10–12. Rest 90 seconds between each exercise. Lean back slightly and pull the bar to your upper chest, driving your elbows down, then let it all the way up to a full stretch.
3. **Low-to-high cable fly**, alternated with 4 — 3 × 12–15. Cables set low, step forward, and sweep your hands up and together to chin height, arms slightly bent. Lower to a full stretch.
4. **Wide-grip seated cable row** — 3 × 10–12. Rest 75 seconds between each exercise. Pull the bar to your upper chest with your elbows out, squeeze your upper back, then reach forward to a full stretch.
5. **Reverse pec-deck**, 3 × 15–20, back to back with 6. Face the machine, arms straight out in front, and sweep them back until they're level with your body.
6. **Cable crunch**, 3 × 10–15. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. Kneel facing a high cable, the rope held either side of your head. Curl your ribs down towards your hips, so your back rounds and your hips stay still, then uncurl slowly. Add weight when 15 is easy.
7. **Dumbbell lateral raise** — 4 × 12–20. Rest 60 seconds. Lean slightly forward, raise the dumbbells out to the sides to shoulder height, elbows soft, and lower slowly. On the last set, when you reach failure, pick up dumbbells about 30% lighter and go again.
8. **Side neck raise**, 3 × 12–15 each side, back to back with 9. Lie on your side on a bench with your head off the end. Lower your ear slowly towards the floor, then lift it towards the ceiling. No weight to start; when 15 is easy, hold a light plate against the side of your head. Never jerk it.
9. **Medicine-ball drops**, 3 × 8. Rest 45 seconds after each pair. Lie on your back, knees bent, a 3 kg ball held at arm's length over your stomach. Breathe out hard through your teeth and brace as you let it go; it lands just above your belly button, and you catch it on the bounce. Drops 5 to 8 land a hand's width to each side. Never on the ribs. Go up a kilo when all eight land on a brace that doesn't give, up to 6 kg.

**Then · the nasal run, 30 minutes**

Easy, on the road or a treadmill, breathing in and out through your nose the whole way. If you need your mouth, slow down.

## THURSDAY · LOWER POWER

Sled, sprints and jumps while you're fresh, then the trap bar heavy, heavy shrugs on the same bar and a second, lighter squat, then an ab finish. Friday's rest after it.

**The movement block · 12 minutes** — hips, balance and sprint prep

1. **Easy bike**, 2 minutes.
2. **Leg swings**, 10 each way on each leg. **Hip airplanes**, 2 × 4 each side. **World's greatest stretch**, 3 each side. **A-skips**, 2 × 15 metres: skip forward, driving one knee up to hip height while the other foot paws the ground.
3. **Sprint build-ups**: three runs of 20 metres, at 60%, 75% and 90%.

**The session**

1. **Heavy sled sprint** — 5 × 20 metres. Rest 2 minutes. Load 40–60% of your bodyweight on the sled. Lean in at about 45 degrees and drive the ground backward through the whole foot, 5–7 seconds a run. Faster than that, add weight; slower, take some off.
2. **Flying sprint** — 3 runs in the size block, 4 in the strength and power blocks. Weeks 1 and 2 at 90%, flat out from week 3. Rest 2½ minutes. Jog-build for 15 metres, then run 20 metres absolutely flat out, and walk back. A hamstring twinge on the build-ups ends the sprints for the day.
3. **Jumps** — 4 × 3. Rest 90 seconds. Size block: box jumps, onto a box about knee height, landing soft, stepping down. Strength block: low-box depth jumps — step off a 20 cm box and, the instant your feet touch, jump straight up as high as you can. Power block: depth jumps, the same off a 30–40 cm box.
4. **Trap bar jump** — 4 × 3 at your peak-power load. Rest 90 seconds. Stand inside the bar, dip and jump as high as you can, land soft with bent knees, reset, go again.
5. **Trap bar deadlift** — the table's sets and percentages, after the ramp. Rest 3 minutes. In the power block, do three broad jumps 90 seconds after each double: two-foot jump forward for distance, stick the landing. Stand inside the bar, hips back, chest up, grip the handles and push the floor away until hips and knees lock out together. No hitch, no leaning back.
6. **Trap bar shrug** — 3 × 8–12. Rest 75 seconds. Straight after the deadlift, on the same bar. Stand inside the trap bar, arms straight. Shrug straight up towards your ears, hold for a second, then lower to a full stretch. Never roll the shoulders.
7. **Pause squat** — size 3 × 5 @ 65%, strength 3 × 3 @ 72.5%, power 3 × 2 @ 75%, all of your squat working max. Rest 2 minutes. Monday's squat with a dead stop at the bottom for two seconds, staying tight, then up as fast as the bar will move.
8. **Nordic curl** — 3 × 4–6. Rest 90 seconds. Kneel with your heels anchored under a pad. Keep your body straight from knees to head and lower yourself forward as slowly as you can, catch yourself with your hands, and push back up.
9. **Copenhagen plank** — 2 × 20–30 seconds each side. Rest 30 seconds. Lie on your side, propped on your forearm, your top leg's knee (or, at level 2, ankle) on a bench and the bottom leg underneath. Lift your hips until your body is a straight line, and hold. It cut groin problems by 41%, and it trains the side of the trunk.

Before Optimal 8 · Move the finish was the side plank, 2 × 30–45 seconds each side, back to back with the dead bug, 2 × 10 each side, 30 seconds after each pair.

## SATURDAY · UPPER POWER

Throws, bench throws and the landmine push press while you're fresh from Friday's rest, then bench and back for size, an ab finish, then the 4 × 4. About 98 minutes.

**The movement block · 10 minutes** — mid-back, shoulders and Achilles

1. **Elbow to the ceiling**, 6 each side. **Cable external rotation at shoulder height**, 2 × 10–12 each arm. **Push-up plus**, 10.
2. **Single-leg calf hold**, 3 × 30 seconds each leg, heavy enough that 30 seconds is hard.
3. Two easy throws of each kind.

**The session**

1. **Medicine-ball chest pass**, alternated with 2 — 3 × 5. Rest 60 seconds between each. Stand a metre from a solid wall with a 4–6 kg ball at your chest and throw it into the wall as hard and fast as you can, then catch the rebound. This is training, not a test, so the wall is fine.
2. **Rotational scoop throw** — 3 × 4 each side. Side-on to the wall, ball at your back hip. Drive off your back foot and turn your hips through to throw it into the wall.
3. **Bench throw** — 4 × 3 at your peak-power load. Rest 2 minutes. Smith machine, with its safety stops set so the bar rests a couple of centimetres above your chest. Lower the bar to the stops, throw it so hard it leaves your hands, catch it with soft elbows and lower it back to the stops.
4. **One-arm landmine push press** — size 4 × 5 each side, strength and power 5 × 3, every rep as fast as you can. Rest 2 minutes. The landmine press with a leg drive: the end of the bar at your shoulder in one hand, split stance, the opposite foot forward. A quick dip of the knees, then drive hard with the legs and punch the bar up and forward to a straight arm. Lower it under control and reset. A rep clearly slower than the first ends the set. When every rep of every set is fast, add the smallest step next time. Log the weight you add to the bar.
5. **Bench press, touch and go**, alternated with 6 — size 3 × 8 @ 70%, strength 3 × 6 @ 75%, power 3 × 5 @ 75%, fast. Rest 90 seconds between each exercise. Tuesday's bench without the pause: lower under control, touch your chest and press.
6. **Pull-up** — 3 × 8–10, with weight on a belt once 10 is easy. Palms facing away, hands just outside shoulder width. From a dead hang, pull until your chin is over the bar.
7. **One-arm dumbbell row** — 3 × 8–12 each side. Rest 60 seconds. One knee and one hand on a bench, the dumbbell hanging at full stretch. Row it to your hip, then lower slowly.
8. **Cable lateral raise** — 3 × 15–20. Rest 60 seconds. Cable at hand height, the handle in your far hand with the cable behind your back. Raise your arm out to shoulder height and lower slowly. On the last set, at failure, drop the weight by about 30% and go again.
9. **Side plank** — 2 × 30–45 seconds each side. Rest 30 seconds. Lie on your side propped on your forearm, elbow under your shoulder. Lift your hips so you're straight from head to feet, and hold. Then the other side.
10. **Landmine rotation** — 3 × 6–8 each side, controlled. Rest 60 seconds. One end of a barbell in a landmine or a corner, the other end at arm's length in front of your chest, feet wide. Swing it down to one hip by turning your hips and pivoting your back foot, arms straight, then across to the other side, trunk braced. Start with the bar plus 5–10 kg.

**Then · the 4 × 4 intervals, about 30 minutes**

On the bike: 5 minutes easy, then four hard efforts of 4 minutes with 3 minutes easy between, then 3 minutes easy. The timer runs all of it. The heart rates and what to log are in The engine, below.

## SUNDAY · PHYSIQUE B

Shoulders, traps, abs, arms and calves for size, and the Pallof press, then a 30-minute nasal run and the weekly check.

**The movement block · 10 minutes** — the whole body

1. **Turkish get-up**, 2 each side, slow, 3 seconds in every position. **Suitcase carry**, 2 × 30 metres each hand.
2. **Band pull-apart**, 15, then a light set of exercises 1 and 5.

**The session**

1. **Cable lateral raise** — 4 × 12–20. Rest 60 seconds. Cable at hand height, the handle in your far hand with the cable behind your back. Raise your arm out to shoulder height, leading with the elbow, and lower slowly. On the last set, at failure, drop the weight by about 30% and go again.
2. **Face pull** — 3 × 15–20. Rest 60 seconds. Rope on a cable at forehead height. Pull the rope towards your forehead, hands apart and elbows high, and squeeze the backs of your shoulders.
3. **Dumbbell shrug**, 3 × 10–15, back to back with 4. Heavy dumbbells at your sides. Lift your shoulders straight up towards your ears, hold for a second, then lower to a full stretch.
4. **Cable crunch**, 3 × 10–15. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. Kneel facing a high cable, the rope held either side of your head. Curl your ribs down towards your hips, so your back rounds and your hips stay still, then uncurl slowly. Add weight when 15 is easy.
5. **EZ-bar curl**, alternated with 6 — 3 × 8–12. Elbows pinned at your sides. Curl up, then take two seconds to lower to straight arms.
6. **Overhead cable triceps extension** — 3 × 10–15. Rest 60 seconds between each exercise. A rope on a high cable with your back to it, or one dumbbell held in both hands. Elbows pointing at the ceiling, lower your hands behind your head to a deep stretch, then straighten your arms.
7. **Incline dumbbell curl**, alternated with 8 — 3 × 10–12. Bench at 45 degrees, arms hanging straight down behind your body: the stretch is the point. Curl without letting your elbows move forward.
8. **Dumbbell skull crusher** — 3 × 10–12. Rest 60 seconds between each exercise. Lie on a flat bench, dumbbells above your shoulders, palms facing each other. Bend only at the elbows to lower the dumbbells beside your head to a deep stretch, then straighten.
9. **Hammer curl** — 2 × 10–12. Rest 60 seconds. Thumbs up, elbows at your sides. Curl, then lower slowly.
10. **Back extension** — Optimal 8 · Move's, by its phase. Rest 60 seconds. On the 45-degree back extension: weeks 1–4, a hold, 3 × 30 seconds, body straight; weeks 5–8, 2 × 12 through the full range, curling down one bit of the spine at a time and rising to a straight line; weeks 9–12, the Jefferson curl, 2 × 8, once its gate is passed. Its test weeks: the back hold, as long as you can.
11. **Seated calf raise**, 3 × 12–15, back to back with 12. Pad on your knees. Lower your heels to a full stretch and hold for two seconds, then rise all the way up.
12. **Pallof press**, 3 × 10 each side. Rest 60 seconds after each pair. A band or cable at chest height beside you, standing side-on. Hold the handle at your chest, press your hands straight out and hold two seconds without letting it twist you, then bring them back. When 10 is easy, stand further from the anchor.

**Then · the nasal run, 30 minutes**

Easy, breathing through your nose only, as on Wednesday.

**The weekly check.** Bodyweight, waist, resting heart rate, the morning colours, your joints and your lower back, sleep, lights-out, energy and your evenings of RANGE or a movement session — Prep's weekly check. In Optimal 8 · Move's test weeks, its fifteen standards too. In weeks 4, 8 and 12 it takes your arm and shoulders too, and the app asks for the photos.

## THE ENGINE

Two easy nasal runs and one hard interval session hold your engine where it is for all twelve weeks, and Tuesday's bursts and pressure rounds add a little power, repeat-effort fitness and fight pace on top. Intensity is what holds it, so the hard session has to stay genuinely hard.

**The nasal runs · Wednesday and Sunday, 30 minutes**

- Breathe in and out through your nose the whole way. If you need your mouth, slow down.
- Heart rate around 65–75% of your peak: the easy zone the app gives you. Flat ground or a treadmill.
- Never on Monday, Thursday or Saturday. The runs stay off the heavy and fast days.
- Log the distance and your average heart rate.

**The 4 × 4 · Saturday, after the session**

1. 5 minutes easy on the bike.
2. Four efforts of 4 minutes at 90–95% of your peak heart rate. The first minute climbs into that band; the last three sit in it.
3. 3 minutes easy between efforts, at about 70%.
4. 3 minutes easy to finish.

Use the same bike every week. Log the total on its screen at the end and your peak heart rate from the Polar. Progress is more on the screen at the same peak heart rate, or the same at a lower one. In the lighter weeks it's 3 × 4, read only against other 3 × 4s, and in test week the 4 × 4 is the engine test.

**The bursts · Tuesday, after the session**

Eight 6-second bursts on the assault bike, 40 seconds easy spinning between, 2 minutes easy before and after: about 10 minutes, and the timer runs all of it. Log the total on the screen at the end and your peak heart rate. Four in the lighter weeks and test week, read only against other fours.

**The pressure rounds · Tuesday, straight after the bursts**

Three rounds of 3 minutes on the same assault bike, with 1 minute's rest between. In each round, a 5-second burst all out every 20 seconds, nine a round, and a steady pace between them, never stopping. A minute easy before and 2 minutes easy after: about 15 minutes, and the timer runs all of it. Clear the bike's screen after the bursts, then log its total at the end and your peak heart rate. Two rounds in the lighter weeks and test week, read only against other twos.

They're the fight sim: bursts with seconds to recover, the way you fight. They cost the power work a little, so they're watched. If Thursday's sled times get slower at the same load, or your jump and throw sets keep ending early, two weeks running, the pressure rounds come out for the rest of the twelve weeks.

## THE RULES

These protect the work, and none of them is optional.

1. **Lighter weeks, 4 and 8.** The squat, bench and trap bar are replaced by their profiles in THE PROFILE: five loads, two fast reps each. Trap bar jumps and bench throws get their four-load profiles. Everything else, the jumps, sled and sprints included, is half the sets, rounded up, the 4 × 4 becomes 3 × 4, the bursts become four, and the pressure rounds two.
2. **The morning check.** Yellow: every load on the bar 7% lighter, and the 4 × 4 at 90%. Red: the heavy lifts, the sprints, jumps and throws, the 4 × 4, the bursts and the pressure rounds come off; the size work runs as written. Three red mornings in a week make the next week a lighter week.
3. **Pain.** Anything over 3 out of 10 while you do it, or worse the next morning, stops that exercise for the day; the jumps, sprints and holds have the button for it. Sunday's check runs the tendon gate: Achilles or knee at 3 or more means no reactive jumps the next week, the tendon holds carried on, and once it's back under 3 they restart one stage down for a week; a hamstring at 3 or more means no flying sprints and the Nordics only if they're pain-free; an elbow or a shoulder at 4 or more turns the next week's dips into push-ups and takes Sunday's curls and triceps work off. The same pain two weeks running means a physio.
4. **Sleep.** Friday is off. Aim for seven hours a night, which at a 3 am start means asleep by 8 pm.
5. **Boxing evenings.** If you're still boxing, it's extra load on top of all this. When it tips you into red mornings, the nasal runs go first, because the boxing covers that work.
6. **No true maxes.** Top triples on pins, never singles.
7. **Bloods and blood pressure** before each cycle: full blood count, lipids, liver, kidneys and blood pressure. With what you're running, those are the numbers that matter.

## TEST WEEK AND WHAT TO EXPECT

Week 12 resets every number and shows what the twelve weeks did. Everything not listed below runs at half the sets.

| Day | The tests |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Broad jump, best of three, after the movement block and the box jumps. Then the squat top triple. |
| Tuesday | Bench top triple, then the weighted chin top triple. |
| Thursday | The movement block, 2 sled sprints and 3 × 3 trap bar jumps, then the trap bar top triple. No flying sprints, depth jumps or pause squats. |
| Saturday | The 4 × 4 as the engine test. |
| Sunday | Tape and photos: the same spots and the same light every time. |

**The top triple.** After the ramp on its card, work up in sets of 3, adding 2.5–5% a set, 3 minutes apart. Stop at the set of 3 that's hard but leaves one or two in you, or when the Spleeft beeps. That weight × 1.08, rounded to 2.5 kg, is your new working max — the app saves it, and it's the camp's working weight too if a fight comes next. For chins, record the heaviest added weight for a clean 3.

**The broad jump.** Two-foot jump forward from a line, stick the landing, and measure from the line to the back of your heels. Open floor, never towards a wall.

**What counts as progress**

- Higher working maxes on the squat, bench and trap bar.
- A longer broad jump, and heavier peak-power loads in the profiles.
- Arms and shoulders up on the tape, with the waist the same or smaller.
- The same or more on the bike's screen in the 4 × 4, at the same peak heart rate.

The next cycle starts from the new numbers, with one tilt towards whatever moved least: one quality gets more, paid for by another, and the engine work never changes.

**What to expect, honestly**

- The first cycle moves fastest, because what time off took comes back quicker than it was built.
- After that, at 45 and already trained, the big lifts move a few per cent a cycle, not tens. That's a good result, not a failure.
- Power is the hardest quality to raise at your age, and it fades fastest. That's why the fast work runs in every block, never just the last one.
- The programme gives the stimulus; food and sleep make the look. Short nights will cost you more than any set or rep in here.

## WHY IT'S BUILT THIS WAY

Every choice below rests on trials where trials exist. Where it rests on coaching practice instead, it says so.

- **Planned variation, all three qualities every week.** Periodised training built more strength than unvaried training, and programmes that varied the load within the week did best (Williams, 2017). Hence the size, strength and power blocks, with every lift heavy on one day and lighter or faster on another.
- **Each lift twice a week, 10–20 hard sets per muscle.** Strength rises with how often a lift is trained. Strength and size both rise with weekly sets, but the returns flatten sooner for strength (Pelland, 2026). So the heavy lifts stay low in sets, and the extra sets go to size, with shoulders, chest, back and arms getting the most.
- **Bar speed ends the heavy sets.** Stopping squat sets at a 20% slowdown built the same strength as grinding on to 40%, and raised the jump 9.5% against 3.5%, with 40% fewer reps. Grinding built more thigh muscle, which is why the size work goes closer to failure and the heavy work doesn't (Pareja-Blanco, 2017).
- **Three minutes between heavy sets.** In trained men, 3 minutes' rest built more strength and size than 1 minute: the squat rose 15.2% against 7.6% (Schoenfeld, 2016).
- **Power built on strength, by several methods at once.** Power needs strength first, then throws, loaded jumps and rebound jumps on top, with the most work going to the weakest link (Cormie, 2011; Jiménez-Reyes, 2017). Loaded jumps and throws stand in for cleans and snatches: the same intent to move fast, without years of technique or the wrist risk. That last part is a coaching call, not a trial.
- **The sled, five runs.** Heavy sled sprints improve the first metres of a sprint and the forward push (Alcaraz, 2018; Morin, 2017). A sled run has no landing, so it's the power work that costs the joints least, and five runs is still well under the ten, twice a week, that Morin's trial used.
- **Two jump days.** Thursday carries the main jumps; Monday's box jumps, before the squat, make a second day. Box jumps land the softest of all the jumps, and a few fast jumps sharpen the heavy sets that follow. Two short jump days is coaching practice, and the Sunday tendon check still governs the rebound jumps.
- **Rotation twice a week, and a trunk that holds.** The legs make a punch's force; the trunk turns it and carries it to the fist. Tuesday's shot-put and hook throws and Saturday's scoop throw train that turn fast, the straight and the hook; the landmine rotation, Tuesday and Saturday, trains it under load; and the Pallof press trains the trunk to hold against a twist. That rests on how the punch works and on coaching practice, not a trial.
- **Abs, traps, rear delts and the neck.** Abs grow like any other muscle, on load and hard sets: 12 a week from the leg raises, the ab wheel and the cable crunches, which take weight. On top, Thursday and Saturday finish the lifting with trunk work that's never taken to failure: the Copenhagen plank on Thursday, and side planks then landmine rotations on Saturday. The traps get heavy shrugs twice a week, the rear delts direct work three times and the neck its own work twice. That's a coaching call for the build you're after, not a trial.
- **Pressing up and forward, not straight overhead.** A bar can only finish straight overhead if the shoulders reach all the way up. When they're stiff, the lower back arches to make up the difference, with the bar's weight on it. The landmine's bar travels up and forward, so both presses finish in front of you. The barbell push press produced as much power as a jump squat (Lake, 2014), and one arm moves less weight, so some leg power is given up; Thursday's jumps, sled and sprints cover it. The swap is a coaching call from how the lifts work, not a trial.
- **A neck trained in every direction, and a brace that fires on time.** Monday trains the neck forward and back, Wednesday side to side. In pooled trials, neck training with a load made the neck stronger bending forward and to the side (Ivanic, 2024). No trial yet shows that a stronger neck prevents concussion, but a 2025 expert consensus sets out how to train it for the knocks the head takes in sport (Fownes-Walpole, 2025). Wednesday's medicine-ball drops train the trunk to brace the instant something lands on it. No study backs the drops; they're long-standing coaching practice.
- **Exercises chosen for the stretch.** Overhead triceps extensions grew the triceps 1.4 times as much as pushdowns (Maeo, 2023). Seated leg curls grew the hamstrings more than lying ones, 14% against 9% (Maeo, 2021). The incline curls, deep presses and full-stretch raises follow the same idea.
- **The engine held, not built.** Endurance can be held for up to 15 weeks on as little as two sessions a week, as long as the intensity stays (Spiering, 2021). The 4 × 4 at 90–95% of peak heart rate raised fitness more than steady or threshold work (Helgerud, 2007). Cardio barely holds back strength or muscle size, but it does blunt explosive power, mostly when both are in the same session (Schumann, 2022). So there's one long hard session, not two; the bursts and the pressure rounds are 25 minutes on an upper-body day, two days clear of Thursday's sprints and jumps; and the runs are easy and kept off the fast days.
- **The pressure rounds: three, not six.** Even a 3 × 2 fight is mostly aerobic, about 77% of its energy (Davis, 2014), but it's fought in short bursts with seconds between them: elite boxers work at about 18 to 1 activity to rest, novices at 9 to 1 (Slimani, 2017). The pressure rounds copy that on the bike. What conditioning costs power and size grows with how often and how long it is (Wilson, 2012), so it's three rounds, on one day. The full fight sim comes in the camp.
- **Hard days and easy days.** Heavy and fast sessions alternate with size work and easy running, and upper and lower body each get a heavy day and a fast day, as the conjugate method splits them. That shape comes from coaching practice — Charlie Francis's sprinters, Westside's lifters — more than from trials.
