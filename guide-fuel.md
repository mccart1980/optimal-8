# THE FOOD GUIDE — PLAIN ENGLISH

## WHAT TO DO TODAY
Open the app. It shows today's feeds in order with a time on each. Eat what the row says at the time it says. Tap it. That's the whole system.

## THE DAY, IN ORDER
1. **Twenty minutes before you train:** half the bottle and a banana. Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday: stir 15 g of collagen into the bottle and have a small orange juice with it.
2. **Straight after:** the other half of the bottle, then the porridge from the thermos on the way to work.
3. **First break:** batch, or a wrap or bagel if you can't sit down.
4. **Lunch:** the same again.
5. **Three o'clock:** two bananas, or a bagel.
6. **Five o'clock, Tuesday, Friday and Saturday:** the top-up — a rice pouch, a banana and honey. **This is the one that never moves.** It's loading tomorrow's session.
7. **Dinner:** steak and eggs on the heavy days, chicken on the others.
8. **Before bed** on the early-dinner nights: casein in water.

Friday has no session, so no bottle — porridge when you wake, then the same day. Saturday and Sunday: porridge at half six, half the bottle at quarter to eight, the other half with two bananas straight after the session, then the feeds through the day.

## SIT DOWN OR ON THE MOVE
Every feed has a swap that matches it. Tap the feed to see them. Can't sit down at break? Wraps or a bagel instead of the tub. Can't face porridge? An egg bagel. The numbers hold whichever you pick. Pick by the slot, not by the day.

## THE PHASE — WHY THE APP SOMETIMES ADDS OR REMOVES A FEED
The training runs in blocks and the food follows it. The app knows which block you're in and changes the day for you:
- **Build** (the first weeks of Prep): an extra top-up on Monday and Thursday. You're being built; eat it.
- **Heavy, fast, camp, fight week:** the normal day.
- **Test week and the last two weeks before a fight:** the normal day, and **do not eat less** because you're training less. That's how people arrive empty.
- **The two weeks after a fight:** three of the 3pm feeds come off. Rest-week food.
- **Making weight:** only if the app's switch is on, and only when it has to be.

If a feed appears or disappears, that's why. Don't add anything back.

## THE BATCH
Cook the mince pot and the sweet potato separately. Weigh each once, cooked, in the COOK tab: type what went in raw and what it weighs cooked, and it tells you how many grams is one portion. Serve by those two numbers from then on.

## DRINKING
The alarms. **The litre bottle with a sachet at work start, empty by nine** — that's the one that matters. Five hundred with each feed, three hundred in the gaps, nothing big after eight. Check the colour at ten and four: pale straw is right; dark means drink now and a second sachet today.

## THE TAPE DECIDES
Weigh on Sunday. Tape every four to six weeks: arms, shoulders, waist. The app's Referee screen tells you if anything needs to change: waist up faster than arms means cut a banana; nothing moving with the waist flat means add a top-up; bodyweight falling more than half a kilo a week means eat more, whatever your appetite says.

## WORDS YOU'LL SEE
**Top-up** — the 5pm carb feed that loads tomorrow. **Batch / batch big** — the mince and sweet potato tub, standard or larger. **The bottle** — the UFIT 50 g, split around the session. **Casein** — slow protein before bed. **Surplus** — eating a bit more than you burn, on purpose, to build. **Phase** — which block of the training you're in. **Sachet** — the electrolytes.

## FIGHT DAY
Weighed in light? Drink a litre and a half for every kilo you lost, with a sachet in every litre, sipped. Eat plain carbs — rice, bagels, bananas, rice cakes and honey — every hour or two until three hours out. Three hours out: a pouch, chicken, a banana. One hour out: half the bottle and a banana. Nothing new on fight day. Caffeine an hour before the bell only if you've used it in camp and your blood pressure's normal.

## THREE RULES
1. The bottle round every session.
2. The 5pm loads never move.
3. Eat every feed, whether you're hungry or not. The plan is the plan; hunger isn't the referee, the tape is.
